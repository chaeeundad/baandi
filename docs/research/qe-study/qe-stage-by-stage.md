FFT calls: 964 (si8). The density needs ten pair transforms per SCF iteration (inverse only, 120 in all) and the potential about four dense transforms per iteration (about 50), which leaves about 800 transforms for the Hamiltonian, i.e. about 400 pairs and about **800 H vectors** (si8, D). QE per H vector: h_psi 0.18 s / 800 = **0.22 ms**, of which the FFT part is 0.135 ms per transform of the 36^3 box. BAANDI (real path, strict, native, T=1, main 72574f9, D0 relayed shares applied to the unsampled 3.13 s): local FFT bucket 31.9 percent = 1.00 s, nonlocal 3.0 percent = 0.09 s, algebra 48.3 percent = 1.51 s, residual and corrections 1.0 percent, other 15.5 percent, for 2014 H vectors: **0.54 ms per H vector** (D, 2.4 times QE) and an algebra time about 30 times QE's 0.05 s. BAANDI standard profile reaches 772 to 864 H vectors on the unperturbed trajectory, which is the QE count; the median perturbed trajectory needs about 1140 (P29A, M). So at equal vector counts the wall gap is cost per vector and algebra, not step count; at median trajectories the count is 1.4 times QE too.

Gap to QE real Gamma at one core, equal delivered-accuracy class (relayed batch-7 D, M): standard 2.80 / 3.75 / 4.40 for si8 / si16 / si32, strict class 5.37 / 6.09 / 14.51. At eight cores (D from relayed tables, QE np=8 valid only for si16 and si32): si16 3.129 s (BAANDI standard, best of real on/off) against QE 0.556 s is 5.6; si32 14.83 s against 1.693 s is 8.8. QE's own scaling from 1 to 8 ranks is 2.5 (si16) and 3.0 (si32); BAANDI's best-of is 1.7 and 1.5. The gap grows with system size and with core count, which is the signature of an algebra and a decomposition problem, not of a constant FFT factor (section 6).

Scaling in N (plane waves), nbnd = b, nk (the reason the gap grows): per SCF iteration the FFT work is O(b N_r log N_r) with N_r proportional to N; the Davidson algebra is O(N b^2) (subspace size and block width both proportional to b); the dense small solves are O(b^3) per step. With b and N both growing with atom count, algebra grows like atoms^3 and FFT like atoms^2 log atoms. QE's algebra share indeed rises from 11 to 18 percent (si8 to si16). A solver whose algebra constant is 30 times larger sees its share and the wall gap grow faster; this matches 2.8, 3.75, 4.4.

## 1. Input, setup, grid, tables, starting point

What QE does. `setup` fixes the order of everything: the number of bands, the first diagonalization threshold, the cutoffs of the smooth and dense spheres, the symmetry operations, the k points and, last, the parallel layout. Defaults that matter for performance and that BAANDI's comparison runs overrode: the band count of an insulator is half the electron count (no empty bands; a metal gets 20 percent more, at least four); the first threshold is 1e-2 Ry for an atomic starting potential and 1e-5 for one read from file; for the second and later ionic steps of a relaxation the driver resets it to 1e-6 Ry (the density is then nearly converged). The dense sphere has four times the wavefunction cutoff for norm-conserving data, so one grid serves everything and no interpolation between grids happens (S section 3.1).

Grid and G vectors. The box edge on each axis is twice the largest Miller index found inside the density sphere plus one, rounded up to the next length whose prime factors are only 2, 3 and 5 (7 and 11 only for IBM ESSL). If symmetry operations with fractional translations are kept, the edges are also raised to multiples of the translation denominators so that the translations map the grid onto itself, unless the user asks for all translations to be used. This is a point where BAANDI and QE differ visibly: BAANDI forced a 32^3 box to make a diamond quarter translation a grid permutation (P19A/P34A), while the relayed QE runs show a 25^3 box with 48 operations of which 24 have fractional translations (a box edge of 25 is not a multiple of 4, so the run either used the all-translations switch or the factor rule did not apply to those translations; the relayed summary does not record the input switches, unresolved). QE applies the symmetry in G space with exact phase factors, so the grid does not have to permute (section 5). G vectors are generated in a half-space order (sphere cut at integer triples) and sorted by |G|^2 with ties resolved within a tolerance; the wavefunction list at a k point is the subset with |k+G|^2 below the cutoff, found by a scan that stops early once |G| exceeds the largest possible value and then sorted by |k+G|^2 (not sorted at k = 0 because the order is already right). Sticks (columns of the grid containing at least one sphere point) are built from this sphere; for one process they only define which columns are transformed (H section 1).

Radial tables. In 7.6 every radial transform that depends on |G| or |k+G| goes through a one-dimensional table with step 0.01 inverse Bohr and four-point Lagrange interpolation: the local potential (the integral is taken after removing the long-range erf tail analytically, and the tail is added back per G), the core charge, the atomic valence charge, the projectors, the atomic wavefunctions and the augmentation functions. The local potential and the core charge are evaluated once per G shell (equal |G|^2), not per vector. Consequence: setup cost per G is a table interpolation, and the quadrature cost is independent of cell size and of the number of G (it depends on the table length, about 1100 points for a 30 Ry cutoff, times the mesh length). The interpolation error (order 1e-8 to 1e-9 relative) exceeds BAANDI's 1e-12 Ha gate, so the table itself cannot be adopted as is; BAANDI's exact squared-|G| memo (P28A) gives the same effect on high-symmetry cells and is exact, but it gains nothing on skewed cells where almost every |G|^2 is distinct (cost then proportional to N_G times the mesh).

Structure factors. Three one-dimensional phase tables per atom (one per reciprocal axis, indexed by Miller index) are built once (OpenMP loop); the structure factor of a species at any G is the product of three table entries. The same device builds the projector phases per k. Cost O(N_G nat), no trigonometry per (atom, G) pair.

Starting point. Density: superposition of the atomic valence charges from the pseudopotential file (a section of UPF), transformed through the table, normalised so that G = 0 equals N_e/Omega (S section 2.2). Potential: built from that density. Wavefunctions (`wfcinit` called from `init_run`, for every k point before the SCF loop): the number of starting functions is the larger of the atomic-function count and the band count; the atomic functions come from the file (with a radial table and real harmonics); when the atomic count already covers the bands each function is multiplied by one plus 5 percent of a random complex number, and when bands exceed the atomic count the missing ones are random with amplitude decaying like one over (|k+G|^2 + 1) (the perturbation applies only in the first case, which is si8 and si16: 32 and 64 atomic functions for 20 and 36 bands). Then `rotate_wfc`: H applied to all starting vectors (one block), one dense generalized (real symmetric at Gamma) problem of the starting dimension, the lowest nbnd pairs kept, rotated back. The first SCF iteration then tells Davidson its input is Ritz vectors (flag `lrot`), which skips the first reduced-matrix build. Cost: one H block of 32 or 64 vectors plus a 32-dimensional solve: at si8 this is about three Davidson steps. The whole `init_run` is 7 to 11 percent of the run (table above).

Cost scaling. Tables: O(table length x mesh) per species. G list and sorting: O(N_G log N_G) once. Structure factors: O(nat N_G). Per k: O(npw x nkb) for the projector matrix (rebuilt every SCF iteration for every k when nk > 1, kept for one k).

BAANDI today. Grid: the same minimum box and 2-3-5 rule (equivalent). Setup after P28A/P28B: engine setup 0.025 s to 0.008 s (si8, Mac O), so the 20.6 percent PROF-1 share is gone on the Mac; dev08 not remeasured. Starting density: uniform (`uniform_density`), starting wavefunctions: lowest-kinetic plane waves, Davidson cold from that (9 and 10 steps in SCF iterations 1 and 2 against QE's 3 and 4). The UPF parser skips the atomic charge and pseudo-wavefunction sections, so neither start exists. The first threshold is a residual-based schedule, not 1e-2 with a first-iteration redo.

What BAANDI could adopt. (a) Atomic starting density from the file's atomic charge, uniform fallback, synthetic analytic atomic charge for the Gaussian fixtures (backlog B10; never tried; changes iteration count, not the fixed point). (b) Starting subspace rotation (free-electron first, atomic later) to skip the cold first iteration. (c) A table or exact shell-sharing for the local potential only for very large or skewed cells; not needed at the benchmark sizes today. (d) For relaxations: ethr-like start of the second ionic step from a tight value and atomic-density extrapolation (stage 7). Expected effect of (a)+(b): the first two SCF iterations carry 4 to 13 percent of the H vectors and the SCF iteration count is 15 against QE's 12 (E: 1 to 3 iterations, 7 to 20 percent of strict work).

## 2. The SCF loop skeleton

What QE does, in order, per iteration (details S section 1): set ethr from the previous error estimate; compute the double-counting integral with the old potential; diagonalize every k; accumulate the density; compute the second double-counting integral; mix; if the first iteration shows that ethr was too loose relative to the new error (error estimate below ethr times max(1, N_e)) tighten ethr and redo the iteration once, without mixing history; if not converged compute the new Hartree and XC potential from the mixed density and the first-order energy correction `descf`; if converged recompute the potential from the OUTPUT density once more (the difference to the previous potential is kept for the force correction) and set `descf` to zero. Convergence is `dr2 < conv_thr`, where dr2 is the Hartree-metric squared norm of the density residual over G != 0 (with the doubling for the half sphere), and conv_thr is in Rydberg: 1e-8 Ry equals 5e-9 Ha (M: the printed estimated accuracy at convergence is 6.6e-9 Ry for si8, 6.3e-9 for si16). The threshold rule is a monotone ratchet min(ethr, 0.1 dr2 / max(1, N_e)) floored at 1e-13 Ry, cap 1e-2 Ry.

What is cheap or clever. The error estimate costs one weighted dot product over the density sphere, which the mixer needs anyway. The ratchet makes the number of Davidson steps per SCF iteration very uneven (1 to 2 steps when the threshold did not change, 11 to 20 when it dropped by 3x or more, S 5.2). The energy bookkeeping adds no transform: eigenvalue sum plus double counting plus the potential energies computed anyway plus descf (S section 6). No separate certificate of eigenvectors or fixed point exists: one iteration with dr2 below conv_thr is the end, and the extra potential evaluation at the end is the only extra work. Run-time counts M: si8 12 iterations at conv_thr 1e-8, 14 at 1e-9, 15 at 1e-10, 18 at 1e-12; si16 10, 12, 13, 15; each tightening step of conv_thr costs about 5 to 10 percent of wall per decade (0.467, 0.513, 0.551 and 0.606 s for si8 at 1e-8, 1e-9, 1e-10 and 1e-12).

BAANDI today. The same sequence with the following differences (S section 7 has the full table): L2 density-residual schedule without ratchet or redo (the standard profile adds a Ritz-change stop and a ratchet on top, uncertified); a full per-iteration energy audit (Hartree and XC of the output density, local energy transform, per-band nonlocal energy: 6.4 percent of the si8 T=8 wall, 9.0 percent for si16, serial); strict mode ends with a certificate solve (68 of 408 Davidson steps on si8, 8.4 percent of H vectors, Mac O). SCF iteration counts M: strict 15 (si8), 12 to 14 (si16); standard 15 on the unperturbed trajectory but 24.5 median (20 to 34) over ten 1e-10 start perturbations for si8 and 20 median (17 to 24) for si16, against 12 and 10 for QE (P29A relayed, M). Failed attempts on the stopping side are recorded (A15, A21, P29A-2, P29B-2/3).

What BAANDI could adopt. The new angle is not another tolerance factor (A21 and the P29 guards failed) but the mixer's start and metric: QE reaches 10 to 12 iterations from an atomic start with a Hartree-weighted history of 8 and beta 0.7, from which a 3 to 4 step first iteration follows. The P17A-5 Hartree-metric test used single unperturbed trajectories (no change in counts); the perturbation harness of P29A measures the distribution, where stagnation events appear in 32 of 100 runs, and has not been run with the Hartree metric, with an atomic start or with beta and history values closer to QE's. That is the premise change that reopens B16 (the metric) and B10 (the start) without repeating A21: the claim to test is that the noise tolerance and the start quality determine the stagnation events, not the tolerance factor. Cheap energy in non-final iterations (descf style, full audit kept for the converged iteration and the certificate) is the S-7 item; it changes the per-iteration energy history only.

## 3. Hamiltonian application

What QE does. Per block of m unconverged vectors: kinetic term (diagonal multiply, OpenMP loop, padding cleared); local potential through FFT with two real bands per transform in the Gamma path (pair packing and parity separation, G section 3); nonlocal term as two real matrix products on the whole block (projection of the block on all projector columns, per-atom coupling on the small projection blocks, back-application accumulated into the output) plus the rank-one G = 0 correction; the imaginary part of the G = 0 output is cleared at the end. FFT count per band: one inverse and one forward per pair, i.e. one transform per band; with MKL-DFTI the full box is transformed (no pruning; the FFTW3 wrapper does prune, H sections 1.8 and 1.9) at 135 us per 36^3 transform on dev08 (M: 964 calls, 0.13 s). There is no band batching on CPU (the batch size is one), the potential is multiplied inside the transform loop as a separate pass, FFT plans are cached per grid shape (a round-robin cache of 20), and the work arrays are allocated once per h_psi call. Cost per band: two transforms per pair plus 4 N nkb real multiply-adds for the nonlocal part: 0.22 ms per band in total at si8 (D), nonlocal 3 to 6 percent of QE's wall.

What is cheap or scales. The FFT part has the same cost in every stage of the run, so the number of H vectors is the lever; the nonlocal part is a GEMM reusing the projector matrix across the whole block (A58b shows BAANDI's explicit band blocking lost on the Xeon, so this is not a gap to chase, see below). The pair trick halves FFT count and vector length, and QE gets it by default at Gamma.

BAANDI today. The Hamiltonian apply of the real path (pair plan over the pruned fused FFT, GEMM nonlocal) shows local FFT 31.9 percent and nonlocal 3.0 percent of working time at T=1 (D0, M), 0.54 ms per H vector (D), 2.4 times QE's per vector although BAANDI prunes (about 45 percent fewer flops than the full box). Two derived clues: (1) the complex path costs 0.61 ms per vector (1.24 s of local FFT for 2044 vectors at T=1, D), so the real pair path gains only about 1.2 times per band where 2 times is expected; (2) MKL's full-box 36^3 transform is 2.8 times faster than rustfft per call on one core (ADR 0019 microbenchmark), which already explains a factor of about 1.6 after pruning. Neither clue is a measurement of the pair path on dev08; the microbenchmark of the pair apply per call on dev08 is the first thing to ask (plan item M1). BAANDI also pays a per-SCF-iteration rebuild of the real Hamiltonian and its 60 FFT pair plans (4.9 percent of the T=8 wall, serial, Mac O; memset about 7 percent of dev08 samples) where QE keeps its plans for the run.

What BAANDI could adopt. (a) Persistent real Hamiltonian and plans (B21, bit-identical, E 3 to 6 percent). (b) FFT kernel parity on x86 for the pair path: measure first; the options are a pruned stick pipeline on the MKL backend (the only way to exceed QE-MKL per band: 55 percent of its flops), better use of rustfft's AVX path, or nothing. (c) No nonlocal change (A43, A58b closed). (d) No band grouping in the FFT (A28; QE does not do it either).

## 4. The iterative eigensolver (regterg for real, cegterg for complex)

What QE does (the structure is in N1 and V; this is the real-path step-by-step with the costs, because it is the decisive stage). State: basis `psi` and products `hpsi` of capacity nvecx = 2 nvec (the default multiplier is 2; the solver refuses less), reduced matrices `hr`, `sr`, coefficient matrix `vr`, Ritz values `e`, a flag per root. Entry: H is applied to the nvec current vectors (even when they are Ritz vectors; at SCF iteration 1 `lrot` skips only the reduced build and small solve). `hr` and `sr` are built whole (nbase = nvec, one real matrix product each of size nbase x nbase over 2 N_half) and, unless `lrot`, solved once (generalized, lowest nvec pairs). Each step then does the following. (1) Pack the coefficient columns of the unconverged roots to the front (this is the only place convergence flags are used). (2) Residual vectors for those roots by two real products over the whole basis: basis times coefficients (scaled by the Ritz value) and `hpsi` times coefficients; width k = notcnv, depth q = nbase. (3) Preconditioner division per root (the diagonal of kinetic, mean local potential and nonlocal diagonal, with the smooth positive denominator of N1 section 2). (4) Norm of each correction (weighted sum with the G = 0 correction), normalise (no orthogonalisation of any kind). (5) H applied to the k new vectors. (6) The new columns of `hr` and of `sr` (for norm-conserving data `sr` is still formed by a product and not taken as the identity) as one rectangle of height nbase + k and width k each, with the transposed part filled by a plain copy loop. (7) Small generalized solve of order nbase + k for the lowest nvec pairs. (8) Per-root test: change of the Ritz value since the previous step below ethr (looser for empty bands: the larger of 5 ethr and 1e-5). (9) If all converged, or nbase + k exceeds nvecx, or 20 steps are reached: form the new eigenvectors by one product of the basis with the first nvec coefficient columns; on a restart also contract `hpsi` the same way (no H application), reset `hr` to the Ritz values and `sr` to the identity and continue with nbase = nvec. Then the driver `c_bands` applies the SCF rule: up to 5 unconverged roots are accepted, more than that repeats the call (at most five repeats), and more than a quarter of the bands (or 5) unconverged after the repeats is an error.

Cost per step in units of one real multiply-add over 2 N_half (about the full-sphere count N) per entry: residual 2 q k, new rectangles 2 (q + k) k, so about 4 q k N; per restart three contractions of q x nvec; small solve O(q^3) (the real generalized driver, range-selected for m < n). Typical si8 values: nvec 20, k about 6.5, nbase 20 to 40, restart every 2 to 3 steps (34 "last" phases in 81 steps). So the algebra work per SCF iteration is about 12 steps x 4 x 30 x 6.5 N = 9e3 N and the small solves cost 0.3 ms each at q about 30.

What is cheap or clever. (i) No object of size N is created for a converged root: its coefficient column is simply skipped (the "soft locking" of V 2.3); converged roots keep contributing through the small problem. (ii) Convergence is decided in the small problem from eigenvalue changes; no residual norm, no N-sized work. (iii) The basis is never orthonormalised; instead the small problem is generalized and its conditioning is guarded by frequent restarts (every 2 to 3 steps, so the basis never exceeds 2 nvec) and by unit-norm corrections; Cholesky failure in the small solve is an error and does not occur in practice (V section 1.3). (iv) Only one rectangle per matrix per step (the mirror part is copied). (v) Restart contraction instead of re-applying H. (vi) The stopping rule is weak by construction (Ritz change, up to five roots unconverged) and the SCF loop, not the solver, supplies the accuracy (P15A replay: 283 to 39 rows at identical per-root numeric thresholds, so the stopping rule explains most of the step gap, ledger A57).

What none of the QE solvers does (V section 3): CG, ParO, RMM and PPCG do not reduce H applications; RMM-DIIS is selected by the input name and runs only a single non-converged sweep in all but iterations 1, 2 and every fifth.

BAANDI today (real path, `real_davidson.rs`; complex `Davidson` similar): block size equal to the band count, capacity max(48, 6 bands) = 120 for 20 bands, orthonormal basis maintained by two projection passes (blocked) and an in-block modified Gram-Schmidt that is serial (6.8 percent of the si8 T=8 wall, 12.2 percent for si16, Mac O); Ritz vectors and products of ALL roots formed every step (the term that dominates tail steps, V 6.2); residual and norm for all roots; incremental reduced H in two rectangles (A55); a full EVD of the live basis (9 percent of the T=8 wall, serial, 232 us per step); contraction restart (A12); strict residual criterion plus certificate or, in the standard profile, the Ritz-change test with a ratchet. Measured shares at T=1 (D0, relayed): algebra 48.3 percent of working time, residual and correction 1.0 percent, zero-fill and copy 12.6 percent of samples (before P33A; memset/memcpy fell after it, -9.6 to -10.7 percent wall, M).

Gap decomposition (D): QE algebra 0.04 to 0.05 s (si8) against BAANDI about 1.5 s: step count 258 against 81 (strict, 3.2 times, stopping-rule definition included), a live basis of about 70 against about 30 columns, all-root versus unconverged-only products, and a full EVD (about 230 us per step on the Mac at T=1) against QE's range-selected small solve (about 0.3 ms per solve on dev08, including its copies). The per-step algebra cost ratio is about 9 (1.5 s / 258 steps = 5.8 ms against 0.05 s / 81 steps = 0.6 ms), times the 3.2 step ratio gives the 30-fold total. Prior attempts (A13 and P31A capacity, A14, A15, A21, A52, P24A) each changed ONE ingredient of a structure whose cost comes from the combination: P31A shows capacity 2 increases steps in BAANDI's orthonormal formulation because each step is expensive and each restart discards memory; QE is cheap enough per step that frequent restarts do not matter. The structural difference is the non-orthonormal generalized formulation with unconverged-only work (backlog B13, "H4" of N1, never attempted; A16 gated).

What BAANDI could adopt. A QE-structured real Davidson as an isolated, switchable module for the real Gamma path (the complex path later): generalized small problem with Cholesky fast path and canonical-orthogonalisation fallback, unit-norm corrections only, capacity as a measured parameter (2, 3 and 6 are all legitimate once steps are cheap, P31A premise), one rectangle per matrix per step, coefficient-column packing so that residuals exist only for unconverged roots, Ritz-change stopping in the standard profile and, in strict mode, one all-root residual by contraction of the stored products plus the existing true-residual certificate. The reopening condition recorded in the backlog (B13: "park unless a dev08 profile after native and standard shows algebra above 40 percent of wall") is met by the real-path D0 profile for strict (48.3 percent at T=1, 57.1 percent at T=8), not yet for the standard profile; the plan asks for that profile (M1). Mandatory stage 0: the rank and conditioning diagnostic on the skewed 20-atom fixture (A52's regression; V 8). Expected effect (E): algebra from 48 percent of wall towards QE's 0.3 times the H-apply time (11 to 20 percent), i.e. a wall factor 0.6 to 0.8 at T=1, with steps possibly rising 10 to 20 percent as P32A's real path did.

## 5. Density, mixing, potential, energy

Density (`sum_band`, gamma branch). Weights come from occupations; the empty-band flag (occupation below 1 percent of full weight) only loosens the eigensolver threshold. Two bands per inverse transform; the increment is w_a Re^2 + w_b Im^2 over Omega. No symmetrisation at Gamma. Then one forward transform of the summed density to G space (half sphere) and one inverse for the real-space copy. For k-point runs the density sum over k, then symmetrisation, then the same two transforms. Cost: b/2 transforms plus two per SCF iteration; si8 0.03 s (8 percent), si16 0.11 s (8 percent).

Symmetrisation (k path). The density is averaged over ALL crystal operations (not only those that permute the k mesh) in G space by stars of G vectors: for each star, the average of the rotated components with the phase of the fractional translation. No FFT and no grid commensurability; cost O(N_G x nsym) per iteration. This is why the relayed QE shifted 4x4x4 run differs from the unsymmetrised one by 1.55e-5 Ha (explained by P34A as a different effective k sampling) and why QE reduces 32 shifted points to 10 with 48 operations at a 25^3 grid. BAANDI's projector is also in G space (`DensitySymmetrizer`) but built over the grid with two full transforms per application, needs the density sphere closed under every operation, and was tied to a grid on which each operation permutes; its default mesh-group mode and the research `density_group = crystal` mode agree with this once the grid permutes (P34A).

Mixing (`mix_rho`, S section 4): history 8, beta 0.7, Hartree-weighted inner products over the density sphere (G != 0, doubled at Gamma), a difference-form Broyden step with a symmetric small solve, G = 0 fixed, optional Thomas-Fermi or local-TF preconditioning (default off). Cost O(history x N_G) per iteration, no FFT except one inverse to regenerate the real-space density: 0.02 s (si8, 5 percent), 0.03 s (si16, 2 percent). BAANDI mixes on the real-space grid (history 6, L2 products over N_r points, about twice the number of density-sphere G vectors at si8), 0.9 ms per iteration (Mac) against QE's 1.7: no wall gap (about 2 percent), but a different metric (backlog B16).

Potential (`v_of_rho`): Hartree entirely in G space (one inverse FFT, half sphere at Gamma, OpenMP loop), LDA XC pointwise in real space, GGA about eight dense transforms (BAANDI twelve). 0.03 s and 0.04 s. BAANDI: two full-box complex transforms of a real field for Hartree and 1.2 ms per iteration (Mac): small.

Energy: eigenvalue sum + double counting + descf (stage 2); no extra Hartree or XC evaluation, no per-band nonlocal energy. BAANDI's per-iteration audit (6.4 to 9.0 percent of the T=8 wall, serial) is the corresponding extra; P28B removed the K-fold repetition for k meshes and shared the Hartree transform (energy phase -17 percent on si8, -88.5 percent on k444s, Mac O), not the audit itself.

What BAANDI could adopt: the cheap non-final energy (S-7/B20 C7), plus overlap of the audit with the next iteration (B22b, bit-identical); QE-style G-space star symmetrisation without a grid restriction would remove the need for the 32^3 grid on the k path (not a performance item, a generality item for symmetric cells on arbitrary grids).

## 6. Parallel and performance design

QE's levels (setup_para): images; pools (k points distributed over MPI groups, each pool holding a whole copy of the plane-wave data); inside a pool the plane-wave level (sticks distributed over ranks, FFT slabs along z, all-to-all transposes inside every transform, distributed matrix products with a sum reduction after each overlap); optional band groups (bands distributed, rarely a win); optional FFT task groups (several bands transformed at once when ranks exceed the grid planes); and a distributed dense eigensolver only when the matrix is large (block 100 or more, at least 2 x 2 processes). Defaults: pools are used ONLY when the rank count exceeds half the number of z planes AND there is more than one k point (then the smallest divisor of the rank count that brings ranks per pool below that limit); task groups only when the ranks of a pool exceed the number of z planes; the rest of the ranks go to the plane-wave level. Consequence: QE's default with N ranks for a single k point (Gamma) is plane-wave decomposition; for a k mesh with N below half the number of z planes it is ALSO plane-wave decomposition and not pools. The relayed shifted 4x4x4 run (grid 25, np=8) gained only 1.7 times over np=1 (0.6225 to 0.3575 s), which is what plane-wave decomposition of a tiny grid gives; the summary does not record whether `-nk` was passed, so QE's best configuration for k meshes (one pool per rank) is unconfirmed and has to be measured against BAANDI (plan item M1).

Threads inside one rank. OpenMP regions in the serial-per-process code are few: the kinetic multiply in h_psi, pack and unpack loops of the FFT helpers, structure-factor tables, the Hartree sum, the copy loops of the dense solver driver and the mixing dot products. The Gamma eigensolver `regterg` and `vloc_psi` themselves contain no OpenMP loops; whatever threads help comes from threaded BLAS and threaded FFT libraries. So there is no band-level threading in QE: "equal cores" in QE means MPI ranks, and QE's multi-thread behaviour is not a benchmark for BAANDI's.

Which regions are serial and why QE scales as it does (M, D): with np = 8 si16 runs 2.5 times faster than np = 1 and si32 3.0 times; si8 is below the timer limit. The serial or poorly parallel parts are the small dense solves (done on one rank and broadcast), the mixing and Hartree over G (parallel over G, but with global sums), and the FFT transposes. QE's speed-up is therefore modest too, but it applies to ALL work, including the algebra, because every product is over the plane-wave index.

BAANDI: a persistent Rayon pool; at one k point the band direction only (pairs and blocks), outer parallelism over k when nk > 1 (inner = floor(T / outer)). At T=8 about two threads are busy (D0: waiting share 84.4 percent, THR-2: 2.2 busy, M and O agree); width limit is structural: 60 percent of H calls carry at most 4 vectors (one or two pairs), GEMMs have 12 fixed row panels and run serial below a 1M multiply-add guard, EVD, in-block MGS, nonlocal GEMM and the per-iteration Hamiltonian build are single-threaded. Phase fit: 0.29 of the T=1 work is plainly serial (THR-1). QE's decomposition over the plane-wave index is the equivalent of splitting every BAANDI operation into row panels, including inside the FFT of one pair (z sticks and xy planes as independent pieces), which BAANDI does not do (B24, revives A34 on a persistent pool).

What BAANDI could adopt: B21 persistent state, B22 overlap of independent phases (nonlocal image with local FFT; audit with next iteration; one rotation region), B23 full-pool inner budget for k solves with a single symmetry map (O: k444s symmetric -5 to -11 percent), B24 plane-group split of one pair's transforms (needs the dev08 wake-latency data), and one decision: the comparison at equal cores must name QE's layout (plane-wave ranks for Gamma, pools for k meshes) and report both.

## 7. End of run

QE after convergence: `forces` (if requested or implied by relaxation or MD) and `stress`, then (if relaxing) the optimizer and the next ionic step. Forces: nonlocal term from the stored wavefunctions and projector derivatives (one more pass over all bands and k points), local-potential and core-charge terms in G space per shell, Ewald, and the SCF correction term built from the difference between the output potential (the extra potential evaluation of stage 2) and the input one; then symmetrisation. Stress: analytic at fixed cutoff: kinetic and nonlocal parts from one more band loop with OpenMP, local, Hartree, XC (with GGA part) and core-correction terms in G space, Ewald; no cutoff scan and no extra SCF (the Pulay stress is a known property of the fixed-cutoff result and the user's problem). Ionic step restart: the default density extrapolation is "atomic" (subtract the old atomic charge, add the new one at the displaced positions), wavefunction extrapolation is "none" but the wavefunctions of the previous ionic step stay in the memory buffer and start the next Davidson; the initial threshold of the next step is 1e-6 Ry; the mixing history is not kept.

Output. The default level for an SCF run is "low": wavefunctions live in memory during the run and are written at the end. `disk_io = nowf` is one level lower: the XML summary and the charge density are written and wavefunctions never are (the next level writes only the summary, the lowest nothing; the mixing history then also stays in memory). With one k point the wavefunctions never go through a buffer; with several, they are copied to and from a memory buffer around each k visit at levels up to "medium" (a memcpy of npw x nbnd per visit and iteration, no disk). `verbosity high` adds per-iteration prints of band energies and timers and does not change the numerics. The timers used in section 0 are QE's own and cover the whole `electrons` call; `PWSCF` wall includes setup and final output.

BAANDI today. Forces and stress are post-SCF phases that exist and are analytic; the stress path additionally runs a cutoff-derivative estimate with extra SCF solves on request (the "Pulay assessment", 58.4 percent of SCF-plus-pass on si8 and 44.6 percent on k444s with it, Mac O); relaxation transfers the density only (no orbitals, no atomic-charge delta, no mixing history; `initialize_density_transfer` documents it) and starts the next ionic step's SCF from the generic schedule. Force time is 28.3 percent of SCF+force on si8 (O).

What BAANDI could adopt: make the cutoff estimate opt-in or computed without extra SCF solves (a reporting choice, not a numerical one); keep orbitals and an atomic-density delta between ionic steps and start the next SCF with a tight first threshold (E: 20 to 40 percent fewer SCF iterations per step; relaxation workload only); compare force and stress timers with QE's `forces`/`stress` clocks on the same cases (plan M1, backlog B19).

## 8. What is clever, in one place

1. The reduction of the eigensolver to the small problem: all decisions (convergence, which roots get work) are taken from the nvecx-sized matrices and eigenvalues; vectors of length N are created only for unconverged roots and at restarts.
2. The whole run applies H to about 800 vectors for si8 (D); an SCF iteration costs 20 vectors at entry plus about 7 per step.
3. One FFT per band at Gamma (pairs), the weak stopping rule, and the energy by double counting make the non-H part of the run about 30 to 50 percent of the time at QE's speed.
4. Everything radial, structural and symmetry-related is shared per shell or per star; table lookup replaces quadrature at the cost of 1e-8 interpolation error.
5. The parallel layout is chosen to avoid communication-heavy levels unless the rank count forces them.

## 9. Facts found in this walk-through that other notes do not contain or state differently

- The random perturbation of atomic starting functions is applied only when the atomic count already covers the band count; extra bands are random functions with a one over (|k+G|^2 + 1) amplitude (S section 2.3 states it for all functions).
- The starting rotation runs for every k point inside `init_run`, before the SCF loop (it is part of the init_run timer, 7 to 11 percent).
- In 7.6 the local potential, core charge, atomic charge, projectors, atomic wavefunctions and augmentation functions are all interpolated from tables of step 0.01 inverse Bohr; the earlier statement that tables are used for projectors only is incomplete.
- Default parallel layout: pools only when ranks exceed half the z planes and nk > 1; this means QE's np=8 k-point run on a small grid is plane-wave decomposed (relayed 1.7 times), so QE's best k-point configuration needs `-nk`.
- OpenMP: no loop inside regterg or the Gamma local-potential routine; the threaded loops are in the kinetic multiply, FFT pack/unpack, structure factors, Hartree sum, mixing dots and the dense-solver driver; the complex cegterg has two collapsed OpenMP loops for the residual and the correction.
- Density symmetrisation averages over all crystal operations by G stars and needs no commensurate grid; fractional translations only raise the box edges to multiples of their denominators when the grid is chosen by the program.
- The second and later ionic steps start with a threshold of 1e-6 Ry and keep the previous wavefunctions in memory; the density extrapolation default is atomic-charge based.
- The converged iteration performs one extra potential evaluation from the output density (kept for the force correction); mixing is skipped there.
- The H-vector count of the relayed si8 real run is about 800, derived from FFT call counts (section 0); BAANDI's standard profile is at 772 to 864 on its unperturbed trajectory.
