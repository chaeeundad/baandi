## Investigations and limitations

Initial exploratory scans failed because the tiny basis could not hold the
requested bands/subspace, and then because a loose initial Ritz solve produced
zero density residual followed by nonzero residual and triggered the existing
growth guard. The final synthetic fixture uses 6 Ha, with strictly resolved
initial Ritz solves in the variable-cell path. No acceptance tolerance was
relaxed. A first final-rebuild attempt also exposed missing symmetry-compatible
FFT sizing; rebuilding now uses the current structure's prepared grid.
Initial clippy failures were fixed before the final gate run.

These are synthetic finite-basis oracles. Si/MgO results and their cutoff differences were unmeasured in the initial
session; see the real-file follow-up for the subsequent measurements. Cartesian fixed-atom constraints reject in
cell modes. `xyz_diagonal` preserves Cartesian orthogonal angles, not arbitrary
skew-cell angles. Scalar backtracking is a local safeguard, not a global-minimum
proof. Frozen/rebuilt differences and P22A's Pulay estimate remain separately
labelled; no cutoff-converged material or experiment/QE agreement is asserted.

README generation is explicitly assigned to a later ticket. Dashboard/session
registry publication belongs to the orchestrator; those paths are outside this
ticket's OWNED_FILES and no external dashboard upload was performed.

## Real-file follow-up: input and frozen-grid corrections

The external directory `/Users/mino/Develop/rust-dft-wt/pseudo/pseudodojo/ex`
was readable, including the actual file contents. SHA-256 matches the P13D
Si/Mg/O records. The earlier missing-env explanation did not establish whether

The real test now reads each P13D material template and changes only external
paths, the uniformly scaled initial lattice, stricter SCF tolerances, and the
relaxation/symmetry sections. Nominal constants are 10.26 Bohr for Si and
7.95 Bohr for MgO. Si uses shifted 4x4x4 sampling and 30/40 Ha; MgO uses shifted
3x3x3 and 50/60 Ha. PBE, density floor 1e-10, Kerker-Pulay, four requested threads,
and the files Si.upf, Mg.upf and O.upf are retained. Each start is +/-2 percent.
The original stress tolerance 1e-7 Ha/Bohr^3 and energy tolerance 1e-9 Ha remain.

Two further failures were exposed by executing the real fixture:

1. Full cell freedom with the shifted primitive-cell mesh preserves its spatial
   **mesh subgroup**, not necessarily the whole cubic crystal group. The first
   exploratory Si trajectory developed a rhombohedral component; it was
   interrupted (exit 130), not accepted as a cubic lattice result. The material
   lattice-constant oracle now explicitly uses isotropic cell freedom. This
   constrains the scientific question to the cubic branch; it does not establish
   full-space-group preservation for unrestricted strains on these shifted meshes.
2. The engine froze its density support, but Hamiltonian setup called
   `basis.density_basis(current_cell)` and rebuilt a nominal-cutoff sphere for
   grid validation. Expansion across a shell caused an erroneous 36-versus-37
   FFT dimension failure. The first isotropic Si compressed run failed after
   77.128 s, retaining its last good structure (not a relaxed result).
   `in_cell_with_frozen_density` now propagates the density reference through
   wavefunction basis construction to this consumer. Ordinary constructors and
   plain `in_cell` retain their original semantics. A shell-crossing regression
   checks exact density Miller ordering and the unchanged grid while reciprocal
   metrics change. No padding, tolerance relaxation, numerical kernel change,
   new dependency or physics-crate edit was used.

Each final case retains input, stdout/stderr, executable/UPF hashes, wall time,
last frozen and rebuilt derivatives and P22A's separate cutoff-derivative Pulay
assessment. The final pressure difference is rebuilt minus frozen, while the
P22A assessment's correction is a stress correction; these are not conflated.
The finite-basis functional differs between starts because each starts with its
own frozen Miller set. Start differences must therefore be reported explicitly,
in addition to the stopping residuals and cutoff dependence. Local dP/da from
the last resolved trajectory step converts the original stress tolerance into
an estimated lattice tolerance for each endpoint; it is not a new relaxed cap
or an independent error bound.

## Periodic rebuild and fresh-reference polish follow-up

The frozen-only discrepancy is not erased or retested with a wider tolerance.
Each initial cell selected a different Miller set and FFT quadrature. At the
common MgO 50 Ha equilibrium near 8.03693534 Bohr, the two initial reference
lengths 7.791 and 8.109 Bohr imply nominal sphere limits scaled to approximately
46.9868 and 50.9007 Ha, respectively. These are effective support limits under
isotropic strain, not new input cutoffs or a claim about the largest sampled G.

The remedy tested here is `rebuild_every=3` and `5`, followed by two fresh-basis
polish cycles, with a third permitted only if the rebuilt pressure still exceeds
the unchanged tolerance. Each polish starts a new optimizer at the previous
accepted geometry with a newly selected nominal-cutoff reference basis and
`rebuild_every=0` within that short cycle. It transfers geometry, not optimizer
memory or an electronic exact checkpoint. Both MgO starts at 50 Ha and both Si
starts at 30 Ha use the original P13D shifted meshes and potentials.

Acceptance now belongs to the **rebuild protocol**, via the ignored
`real_files_rebuild_start_agreement` test and `rebuild_protocol.py`. It requires
all individual stages to converge, final |Pr| < 1e-7 Ha/Bohr^3, and start-point
agreement within the identical previously reported lattice tolerance estimates:
1.0693530915697647e-4 Bohr for MgO 50 Ha and 2.310411789e-4 Bohr for Si 30 Ha
(the exact stored value is read from the original evidence). SCF energy,
density and eigen tolerances remain 1e-11 Ha, 1e-9 and 1e-10 Ha, respectively;
optimizer energy tolerance remains 1e-9 Ha. No estimate is refitted or enlarged.
The original frozen-only test/analysis now reports its finite-basis differences
without asserting that different functions share a minimum. The previous
failed comparison and all its numbers remain in `real-followup/`.
