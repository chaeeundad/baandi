## Objective
Electronic-State Landscape Explorer (ESLE): discover, classify, compare and track multiple self-consistent electronic solutions for a given structure and across related configurations. Two distinct objectives: (1) ground-state candidate search (find several self-consistent solutions, report the lowest found, never claim global optimality); (2) electronic-state tracking along a parameterized path R(lambda) (branch alpha, n_alpha(r; lambda)). Reusing a converged state can bias later calculations toward the same metastable branch; ESLE must address this explicitly. Long-term targets: transition-metal oxides, battery cathodes, defects, alloys, magnetic and correlated systems.

## Scientific requirements
3.1 Multiple SCF solutions: SCF is not assumed to find the global minimum. Initialization strategies (only those compatible with the implemented physics): atomic superposition density, previous converged density, previous wavefunctions when compatible, randomized density perturbation, alternative initial magnetic moments, alternative orbital occupation matrices, controlled perturbation of existing solutions. Spin initialization requires spin-polarized DFT; occupation control needs DFT+U or similar; never simulate these with physically inappropriate approximations.
3.2 Electronic state identity: versioned `ElectronicState` with explicit mandatory, optional and future fields: structure and lattice; pseudopotential ids and hashes; XC functional; k mesh, weights, symmetry; cutoff and basis metadata; electron count and occupations; density; wavefunctions or compact form; eigenvalues and Fermi level; spin density and local moments; occupation matrices; total energy and components; residuals and convergence history; tolerances; solver version and provenance; parent state and initialization method. Serialization versioned and validated.
3.3 State comparison: never by direct wavefunction equality (phase, unitary rotation, band order). Investigate: total energy, density difference, spin-density difference, local moments, occupation patterns, gauge-invariant occupied-subspace comparison, density-matrix or projector distances; account for compatible k meshes, occupations, bases. Define a documented distance with physically meaningful tolerances. Distinct states must not be merged only because energies are similar.
3.4 Branch tracking along R(lambda): record branch identity, parent-child links, similarity, energy evolution, charge localization changes, magnetic changes, possible crossings, disappearance or loss of convergence. Smooth continuation is not proof of lowest-energy branch.
3.5 Independent exploration to mitigate inherited-state bias: policy may combine reused states, fresh atomic initializations, controlled perturbations, alternative spin and occupation patterns, periodic independent multi-start; possible even without detected instability; budget configurable.

## Architecture guidance
Keep the numerical solver independent from the landscape layer. Suggested logical modules (not mandatory crate names; avoid premature crate proliferation; follow the real repository): dft-core (structure, basis, kpoints, hamiltonian, eigensolver, scf); dft-state (electronic_state, serialization, compatibility, provenance); dft-landscape (state_comparison, state_registry, initialization, multi_start, branch_tracking, exploration_policy); dft-validation (reference_comparison, numerical_invariants, regression_tests).
Principles: (1) SCF accepts an explicit initial electronic state when supported; (2) SCF returns a structured result with convergence diagnostics; (3) persistence not embedded in numerical kernels; (4) exploration strategies replaceable without touching the Hamiltonian; (5) CPU and future GPU backends share state semantics; (6) reproducibility and provenance mandatory; (7) ESLE never silently changes the physical model or numerical settings when comparing candidate energies.

## Roadmap
A: finish k-points and the baseline CPU KS-DFT (no ESLE feature may delay it). B: state infrastructure after baseline validation (structured results, metadata, checkpoint serialization, compatible restart, strict compatibility validation, reproducibility tests). C: multi-start SCF with non-spin-polarized initializations, record every attempt including failures, compare states, identify distinct solutions. D: electronic-state continuation across compatible structures (density transfer, normalization, electron count, FFT grid transformation; compare continuation against independent initialization). E: spin, DFT+U, orbital occupation control, magnetic and orbital classification (only after those methods exist). F: adaptive exploration (risk indicators, Bayesian policies, learned initialization selection; any learned policy must beat simple deterministic baselines).

## Validation
Infrastructure systems: Si (deformation and continuation), Al (metallic occupations and k sensitivity), MgO (insulator). Later: bcc Fe, transition-metal oxides, defective oxides, Li cathodes, NCM-related structures. Metrics: number of distinct converged solutions, lowest energy found, total SCF iterations, wall time, time per accepted solution, classification consistency, restart reproducibility, failure rate, sensitivity to initialization and to tolerances. Energy comparisons require identical pseudopotentials, XC, structure, charge, smearing, k sampling and convergence. Different-geometry total energies are distinct from same-geometry state comparisons. A lower-energy solution found elsewhere is evidence that the earlier candidate was not the lowest known. No finite multi-start search proves a global minimum.
Experiments after a prototype: A conventional restart, B independent initialization, C continuation versus exploration, D adaptive search budget.
