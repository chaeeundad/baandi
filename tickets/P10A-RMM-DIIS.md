TASK_ID: P10A-RMM-DIIS
GOAL: An RMM-DIIS (residual minimization with direct inversion in the iterative subspace) eigensolver as an isolated, switchable alternative to the block Davidson solver, aimed at larger systems and larger band counts where Davidson's orthonormalization, subspace diagonalization and memory costs grow. It will probably not pay off on the small 8-band benchmark cases: develop and judge it on a range of band counts (8, 16, 32, 64, 128 or what the machine allows) and system types, and record the crossover point. Read tickets/P10-COMMON.md first, then docs/research/davidson-and-scf-thresholds style notes (docs/research/qe-study/davidson-and-scf-thresholds.md, including its measured outcomes section: the tuning hypotheses that failed for Davidson), crates/dft-solvers/src (Davidson, LinearOperator, Preconditioner, EigenSolution, tests), docs/decisions/0006-eigensolver-api.md, crates/dft-engine/src/scf.rs (how the solver is called per k point, the tolerance schedule, buffer-band policy from ticket P7PC if merged).
OWNED_FILES: crates/dft-solvers/src/rmm.rs (NEW) and crates/dft-solvers/tests/rmm.rs (NEW) with a `mod` line and re-exports in lib.rs (P7PC edits Davidson in lib.rs: touch lib.rs minimally), docs/research/rmm-diis-design.md (NEW), docs/performance/rmm-diis-benchmark.md (NEW), docs/decisions (NEW ADR for the solver selection API if you add one; additive only, the default stays Davidson). Do not touch other crates except an additive solver-selection option in crates/dft-engine/src/scf.rs if needed for the end-to-end comparison (a few lines; say which), manifests, Cargo.lock, tickets/, artifacts/, baselines, README.
CONTENT: (A) analyze what can be reused (operator products, preconditioner, residual evaluation, orthonormalization adapters, per-band tolerances). (B) Study the published RMM-DIIS algorithm (Pulay 1980 DIIS; Kresse and Furthmuller 1996 for the band-by-band residual minimization with preconditioning; public descriptions of the QE and other codes' implementations; do not copy any source of any code; VASP is proprietary: public papers only): band-by-band versus blocked variants, the DIIS history per band, the trial step and its preconditioning, the subspace rotation (Rayleigh-Ritz) that RMM needs before and after, the need to orthonormalize the bands (Cholesky/Loewdin or Gram-Schmidt) and its cost, degenerate manifolds (RMM-DIIS can converge several bands to the same eigenvector: the safeguards), initial-guess quality requirements (it needs a good start: from Davidson iterations or from the previous SCF step), restart and fallback. Distinguish published algorithm from implementation choices and record them. (C) Implement the solver (blocked over bands with per-band DIIS histories, preconditioned residual updates, subspace rotation, orthonormalization, per-band tolerances compatible with the buffer-band policy: strict bands and loose buffer bands), explicit failure reporting (typed errors with per-band residuals), safe restart and a fallback to Davidson (a hybrid policy object: Davidson warm-up then RMM-DIIS refinement, fallback on stagnation or on a detected duplicate eigenvector). (D) Benchmarks (document and script them under docs/performance/): synthetic Hermitian operators with controlled spectra (clustered, degenerate, nearly degenerate, wide spectra, 8 to 128+ wanted bands), operator-count and wall-time comparison with Davidson, residual history, orthogonality error, eigenvalue accuracy against a dense solve, peak memory; synthetic pseudopotential systems and Si supercells with growing band counts through the engine (end to end SCF with the solver switch) with several initial-guess qualities; report the crossover in band count and the variability. (E) Evaluate the hybrid policy and say honestly when it is not faster.
GATES: existing Davidson tests unchanged and green; RMM-DIIS agrees with a dense solve to the tolerance on all test spectra; degenerate bands never falsely converge (test with exactly degenerate clusters and a wanted-set boundary inside a cluster: it must either converge correctly or fail explicitly, never return duplicates); orthogonality within the repository's validated tolerance; SCF with the RMM-DIIS switch gives the same total energy as Davidson within 1e-9 Ha on the benchmark systems and the QE comparison cases (scripts/compare_kpoints_qe.py unchanged and passing with the default solver); deterministic for every thread count; scripts/check.sh green; no new dependency; no unsafe.
DELIVERABLES: the design note, the solver and tests, the benchmark document with the crossover analysis and a recommendation (default stays Davidson; say when to switch), report.
