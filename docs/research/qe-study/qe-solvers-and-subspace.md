## 1. The small dense generalized eigenproblem (cdiaghg and neighbours)

### 1.1 What is solved and which LAPACK routine
Inside Davidson the reduced problem is `H_red c = e S_red c` with Hermitian `H_red` and Hermitian positive definite `S_red`, dimension `n = nbase` (at most `nvecx`, which is 2 times the band count by default). The solver receives `n`, the number `m` of wanted pairs (the band count `nvec`), both matrices with leading dimension `nvecx`, and returns the lowest `m` eigenvalues and eigenvectors.

Two code paths, selected by one test, `m == n`:
- all eigenpairs wanted (`m == n`): the LAPACK driver for the full generalized problem (QR-iteration based, `ZHEGV`). This occurs when the caller wants every vector, for example the dense rotations inside ParO or the RMM subspace rotation.
- otherwise (the normal Davidson case, `m < n`): the range-selected driver `ZHEGVX` with the index range 1..m, eigenvectors requested, absolute tolerance zero (meaning the default tolerance of order machine epsilon times the tridiagonal norm).

Neither path uses a divide-and-conquer driver in the serial LAPACK version. The range-selected driver does, in order: a Cholesky factorization of `S_red` (upper form), reduction to a standard problem by the two-sided triangular transformation, reduction to real tridiagonal form, bisection for the eigenvalues in the index range, inverse iteration for the m eigenvectors of the tridiagonal matrix, back-transformation of those m vectors through the Householder reflectors, and a triangular solve to undo the Cholesky transform. Hence the cost of the part that scales with the number of vectors is `O(n^2 m)` instead of `O(n^3)`, while the tridiagonalization and the Cholesky transform stay `O(n^3)`.

### 1.2 Preservation of inputs, bookkeeping, distribution
- The routine saves the real diagonals of `H_red` and `S_red` before the call, because LAPACK overwrites the matrices, and afterwards restores both matrices from the saved diagonals and the untouched lower triangles (upper triangle rebuilt by conjugation, padding rows zeroed). The Davidson loop relies on this: it keeps appending rows and columns to the same reduced matrices across iterations and never rebuilds the old block.
- Only the root process of a band group solves the problem; eigenvalues and eigenvectors are then broadcast. In a multi-process run the other processes wait. Thread parallelism of this stage exists only through the threaded BLAS inside LAPACK, which is poor for matrices of order 20 to 100.
- There is a distributed alternative (selected only when the orthogonalization sub-group has at least two processes in each direction, input `-ndiag`): Cholesky of `S_red`, explicit inversion of the triangular factor, two Cannon-style distributed matrix multiplications to form `L^-1 H L^-H`, a distributed divide-and-conquer or ELPA/ScaLAPACK eigensolver for all eigenpairs, then a back-transformation. The point of inverting the factor and using multiplications is that every step becomes a matrix multiplication, which distributes well. It is irrelevant for a single process.
- A GPU path calls the vendor dense generalized solver with the same index-range semantics; no further structure.

### 1.3 Safeguards, and what is missing
- There is exactly one numerical guard in the solver: if the Cholesky factorization of `S_red` fails (a non-positive pivot) the run stops with an error naming a non-positive-definite overlap matrix. There is no rank-revealing fallback, no eigenvalue-based truncation, and no restart on failure. The safety comes from upstream: the correction vectors are normalized to unit Euclidean length before they enter the basis (the code comment says this is "to improve numerical stability of the subspace diagonalization"), the diagonals of both reduced matrices are forced to be real after every build, and the basis is dropped back to the `m` Ritz vectors (a restart) whenever it would exceed twice the band count. A basis of at most `2m` columns, restarted frequently, rarely becomes ill conditioned.
- Degenerate eigenvalues inside the wanted range (Si at Gamma has threefold degenerate bands) are handled by inverse iteration with LAPACK's built-in reorthogonalization of vectors belonging to eigenvalues closer than about `1e-3` of the matrix norm, limited to a few passes. This is a known weak point of the inverse-iteration approach: orthogonality of vectors in tight clusters is not guaranteed to machine precision. QE tolerates it because the eigenvectors only feed the next correction and the final contraction, and any residual non-orthogonality is repaired by later steps or by the next SCF iteration; the SCF loop never certifies orthonormality of the returned wavefunctions beyond what the iteration provides.
- The QE RMM code uses a different guard for its tiny history problem (dimension at most the DIIS depth, 4 by default): a canonical orthogonalization. It diagonalizes the overlap of the history vectors, keeps only directions whose overlap eigenvalue exceeds `1e-14`, whitens, diagonalizes the transformed matrix, takes the eigenvector whose eigenvalue has the smallest magnitude, and renormalizes. If at most one direction survives it falls back to the newest vector. This is the principled treatment of near dependence in a non-orthonormal basis and is the relevant model if BAANDI adopts a non-orthonormal Davidson (hypothesis 3).

## 2. Details of the block Davidson that note 1 left open (cegterg)

2.1 **The work of one step is proportional to the number of unconverged roots, not to the band count.** Per step, with `p` basis columns before the step, `k` unconverged roots, `q = p + k`:
- the residual directions are formed by two block multiplications of the stored basis and the stored H-products with the Ritz coefficient columns of the unconverged roots only (the coefficient columns are packed to the front so that these are contiguous multiplications of width `k`); for norm-conserving potentials the "S" product is the basis itself, so the work is `2 N q k` complex multiply-adds (`N` plane waves); for ultrasoft/PAW it is three products;
- the correction is multiplied by the preconditioner, normalized by its Euclidean norm (not an S-norm), H is applied to the `k` new columns (and S for ultrasoft), and then only the new rows of the two reduced matrices are computed: one rectangle of width `q` and height `k` for H and one for the overlap, the remaining block obtained by Hermitian conjugation (`2 N q k` multiply-adds in total);
- there is no orthogonalization, no residual norm, no Ritz vector formation for converged roots.
Altogether about `4 N q k` multiply-adds per step plus the small solve, against an `H_red`-only increment for BAANDI (section 6).

2.2 **Convergence is decided entirely inside the small problem.** After the small solve, the `m` lowest eigenvalues are compared with those of the previous step; each root is declared converged by `|Δe| < ethr` (empty bands `max(5 ethr, 1e-5)`). The declaration is not sticky: it is recomputed from scratch each step, so a root can become unconverged again. No `N`-sized object is touched to take the decision. Ritz vectors of converged roots are not formed until the final contraction.

2.3 **What "locking" means in QE: nothing is locked, but converged roots cost no `N`-sized work.** The converged roots stay in the reduced problem as ordinary basis combinations. The only saving is that no correction is built for them. This is the "soft" version of locking and is made possible by 2.2.

2.4 **The first diagonalization is skipped when the input wavefunctions are already Ritz vectors** (flag set only in the first SCF iteration, immediately after the starting-wavefunction rotation of section 5): the reduced matrix is then diagonal with the Ritz values, the coefficient matrix is the identity and `notcnv = nvec` at entry. In later SCF iterations the old wavefunctions are not Ritz vectors of the new Hamiltonian, so the full sequence runs: H applied to all `nvec` columns, the `nvec`-by-`nvec` projection, one small solve, then iterate. This explains the QE counters in `qe_iteration_data_relayed.md`: H blocks = Davidson steps + one initial block per SCF iteration + one in the starting rotation, that is 83 + 14 + 1 = 98 for si8 at 1e-8, and 116 + 20 + 1 = 137 for the 1e-12 run (both match the relayed call counts exactly). So per SCF iteration QE spends `nvec` H applications at entry and then `k` per step.

2.5 **Iteration cap and tolerated failure.** One call is limited to 20 steps. If it ends with unconverged roots the caller may repeat the call (at most five repeats), but in an SCF run (as opposed to a non-self-consistent run) it only repeats when MORE THAN FIVE roots remain unconverged. Up to five unconverged roots are accepted silently. There is no residual certificate at the end of an SCF. This is a difference in guarantee, not only in speed: BAANDI's strict residual criterion plus certificate (a design requirement of this project) costs steps that QE does not spend.

2.6 **Restart (refresh) mechanics.** Triggered when all roots converge, when `nbase + notcnv` would exceed the capacity, or at the iteration cap. The new first `m` columns are the Ritz vectors (one block product of stored basis and coefficients); the stored H products are contracted the same way; the reduced matrices are reset to the Ritz values and identity. With capacity `2m` and about `m/2` unconverged roots per step, a restart occurs roughly every second step: the relayed profile shows 39 "last" phases in 83 steps for si8. This is effectively a restarted block method with memory of about one step, plus the Ritz vectors; QE therefore leans on its weak stopping criterion and on frequent cheap steps.

2.7 **Dimension rule.** The solver refuses to start unless `nvec <= nvecx/2`: capacity must be at least twice the band count. The documentation says a larger multiplier (try 4) may reduce iterations but costs more in the small solve, and should be used only if the time in the small solve is small relative to the Hamiltonian application. The default 2 is a memory and subspace-cost choice, not a convergence-optimal one.

## 3. The alternative solvers

### 3.1 Conjugate gradient, band by band (ccgdiagg)
Each band is minimized in turn. The current vector is first orthogonalized against all lower bands (two matrix-vector products against up to `m-1` columns, done twice); the gradient is preconditioned by a diagonal approximation and projected orthogonal to the lower bands; a Polak-Ribière conjugate direction is built; and the Rayleigh quotient is minimized exactly in the two-dimensional plane spanned by the current vector and the normalized direction (a closed-form 2x2 rotation: compute the three matrix elements, rotate by the half-angle of the 2x2 problem, choose the lower root). Convergence of a band: change of its eigenvalue below `ethr` (empty bands looser). Up to 20 iterations per band, one H application per band per iteration, on a single vector (bad for FFT batching). Bands are visited in increasing order, so later bands are orthogonalized against all earlier ones, `O(N m^2)` total. The documentation states it is much slower than Davidson but uses less memory and is a little more robust. No subspace is built; there is no small dense solve except an initial subspace rotation of the starting vectors. For BAANDI this is a robustness baseline, not a speed candidate.

### 3.2 ParO (paro_k_new with the block preconditioned CG bpcg_k)
Principle (parallel orbital updating): for each unconverged band `i`, solve approximately the correction equation `(H - e_i S) x_i = -(H - e_i S) psi_i` restricted to the complement of the current band space, then perform ONE Rayleigh-Ritz in the span of the current bands and the corrections, and repeat. The corrections are independent, hence parallel over bands.
- The inner solver is a block preconditioned conjugate gradient on the correction equation. The right-hand side is the residual, the preconditioner is the same diagonal one as for Davidson, each search direction and iterate is projected orthogonal to all current bands by one block product pair against the stored band block (cost `2 N m k`). The inner solve stops per vector when the preconditioned gradient energy-norm has dropped by a factor 100 relative to its initial value (the relative factor `1e-2`, with a floor of `0.01 ethr`), when one step fails to improve the quadratic functional (then the last step is undone), or after five iterations. The relative stopping rule is the design reason: the more accurate the band already is, the more accurately the correction is computed, without a separate schedule. Each inner iteration costs one H application per active band.
- The outer subspace has dimension at most `1.5 m` (a half extra, at least four extra). Number of corrections per outer iteration: about half the number of bands not yet converged is the nominal design, bounded by the free workspace. Bands already converged remain as passive basis vectors; unconverged bands are packed first. The small problem is solved with the full rotation routine (all eigenpairs, `n = m`), and the stored H, S and wavefunction products are rotated with it, so H is not re-applied.
- Convergence test: eigenvalue change below `ethr`, only for the bands that actually received a correction in this iteration. At most 20 outer iterations per call.
- Cost per outer iteration `[E]`: `2-5` H applications per unconverged band (inner CG), projection against the band block `2 N m k` per inner iteration, one rotation of three blocks at order `N (1.5 m)^2`. The number of H applications is NOT lower than Davidson's; the advantage is that the dependency structure is `m` independent chains followed by one small dense step, which suits band-parallel and large-process-count machines. QE documents it as an option in its own right and as the companion of the RMM option. For a single-process or few-thread solver it is not a candidate to replace Davidson on cost grounds.

### 3.3 RMM-DIIS (crmmdiagg, driven by c_bands)
What QE actually runs under the input name `rmm-davidson` (and `rmm-paro`) is a hybrid and is NOT a converged solver:
1. In SCF iterations 1 and 2 and every fifth iteration thereafter the driver calls Davidson (or ParO for `rmm-paro`, at iteration 1, 6, 11, ...). This stabilizes the SCF loop.
2. In the other iterations: one Rayleigh-Ritz rotation of the current wavefunctions in their own span (one H application of the whole block, one projection, one small solve, one rotation of the vectors), then one RMM sweep, then a blocked Gram-Schmidt of the result with the H products carried along (so H is not re-applied), block size 16 by default.
3. The sweep is limited to the DIIS depth (default 4) steps unless the input switch for converged RMM is set; with the default the sweep is performed once and the result is accepted without convergence check (the unconverged count is forced to zero in SCF). The SCF loop itself provides the convergence over iterations. Convergence is checked per band by the same eigenvalue-change criterion and is sticky within the sweep: converged bands are frozen.

One DIIS step per band: (a) build the history of the last up to `d` states and their residuals; (b) compute the overlaps of residuals and of states, assemble two small `d x d` Hermitian matrices per band (one new row/column per step, `d` dot products each, matrix-vector work only); (c) solve the small problem as described in 1.3 (canonical orthogonalization, smallest-magnitude eigenvalue) to obtain the combination coefficients that minimize the residual norm per unit state norm; (d) form the extrapolated state, H-product and residual by the same coefficients; (e) take a preconditioned trial step along the residual; one H application on the trial direction; (f) pick the step length from a quadratic interpolation of the Rayleigh quotient using the value at zero and at a fixed trial length 0.5, clipped to [0.05, 1]; (g) update the state, its H product and the eigenvalue estimate by the same two coefficients. The preconditioner is the Kresse-Furthmüller form: a smooth rational function of the plane-wave kinetic energy divided by 1.5 times the band's own kinetic energy, tending to a constant for low `|k+G|` and decaying inversely at high energies (principle only; see the cited paper in the source comment). It depends on the band through its kinetic energy, unlike the Davidson one which depends on the band through its eigenvalue.

Cost per SCF iteration for `m` bands `[E]`: about `(1 + d) m` H applications (default `d = 4`: about `5 m`; the trial step is the only application per DIIS step), all other work is vector operations and dot products, `O(d N m)` for the sweep, plus the single `O(N m^2)` rotation and the `O(N m^2)` Gram-Schmidt. No growing subspace, no `O(q^3)` solves larger than 4. For `m = 20` this is about 100 H vectors per SCF iteration, comparable to the QE Davidson total of about 76 per iteration (section 6), but with far less non-H work.
When QE recommends it: the documentation describes it as an iterative option that alternates with Davidson/ParO "to stabilize the SCF loop"; its stated use case is larger systems (many bands) where the cost of the Davidson subspace work dominates. No timing guidance is given in the source tree.

### 3.4 PPCG
Not present in QE 7.6 (see status). From the published algorithm (Vecharynski, Yang, Pask): per iteration, residuals of all bands are formed with one block product, preconditioned, and combined with the previous directions; instead of one large Rayleigh-Ritz over `3m` vectors, many small Rayleigh-Ritz problems of size `3 x 3` (or blockwise a few bands) are solved per band section, and a single full Rayleigh-Ritz of dimension `m` is done per iteration for the eigenvalues. The saving is that the dense small problem does not grow with the subspace and its cost is `O(m^2)` rather than `O((2m)^3)` or `O((3m)^3)`. It is a literature option, not a QE finding; mention only because the task asked for it.

### 3.5 Davidson_RCI
It is the same algorithm as the k-point Davidson, restructured with reverse communication: the routine returns to the caller whenever it needs H, S or the preconditioner applied, so that an external library (the electronic structure library interface) controls the operator. Differences noticed: the refresh test is slightly different (the vectors are returned and the basis refreshed only when the basis would overflow; there is no separate refresh at exact convergence beyond the contraction for the output). Algorithmically nothing new. It confirms that the whole Davidson algorithm can be expressed with the operator as a black box that is called with a block of `k` vectors, which BAANDI already does.

### 3.6 Direct diagonalization
For a very large number of bands the dense Hamiltonian in the plane-wave basis is built and all (or the lowest `nbnd`) eigenpairs computed by the dense solver, serial or distributed. The documentation says to use it only when a large number of unoccupied states is needed. It is a cross-check path, relevant to BAANDI only for the already existing small dense baseline.

## 4. The generalized problem with S (norm-conserving versus ultrasoft/PAW)
Structure only. For ultrasoft/PAW the basis keeps a third stored array of overlap-products, the reduced overlap is built from basis times overlap-products instead of basis times basis, the residual of a Ritz pair is `(H - e S) psi` (three block products instead of two), and the final Ritz vectors satisfy a generalized orthonormality `psi^H S psi = 1`. For norm-conserving pseudopotentials `S = 1`, the overlap block is the Gram matrix of the basis, and two things follow that matter for BAANDI:
- the overlap rectangle is one extra block multiplication per step (`N q k`), cheaper than orthonormalization;
- the preconditioner overlap diagonal is 1.
BAANDI's Hamiltonian has `S = 1`. The generalized formulation therefore costs only the Gram rectangle and a Cholesky or canonical orthogonalization in the small problem.

## 5. Band count, empty bands, and the start
- The default band count for an insulator is half the number of electrons. Extra empty bands exist only on request. A band is "empty" (looser threshold) only if its occupation divided by the weight of its k point is below 1 percent; with the default band count for an insulator no band is empty, so every band is converged to the strict threshold. This is a small block with a uniform threshold. (The relayed si8 run used 20 bands, i.e. four extra, so those four use the looser threshold in that run.) The option that forces full accuracy for empty states is off by default; it is needed by workflows that use empty-state eigenvalues (the documentation says it should not affect total energy, forces or other ground-state properties).
- Starting wavefunctions by default are the atomic pseudo-wavefunctions with a small random perturbation, plus random vectors if there are fewer than `nbnd`. Their number is `max(natomwfc, nbnd)`. The start does: one H application to all of them (`nstart` columns), the `nstart x nstart` projection and overlap by block products, one small generalized solve of dimension `nstart` requesting the lowest `nbnd` pairs (range-selected path, because `nbnd < nstart` when there are more atomic functions than bands), one rotation back into the plane-wave basis. For si8 with 8 atoms and four atomic functions each, `nstart = 32` against 20 bands. This is the "first diagonalization" cost: one H block of 32 plus a 32-dimensional solve. The Davidson in the first SCF iteration then starts from Ritz vectors and skips its own first solve (2.4). In later SCF iterations the start is the previous iteration's wavefunctions.
- With the first threshold 1e-2 the first iteration takes few steps in QE (3 for si8) because atomic-like starting vectors are already close.

## 6. Mapping onto BAANDI: where the per-step cost is (estimates)

### 6.1 What the code does (read from the sources)
- Capacity and block. The engine builds the solver with a capacity of `max(48, 6 * bands)` and block size equal to the band count. For 8 bands that is 48. For the 20-band si8 case the same rule gives 120, not 48 (verify in the run; the statement "capacity 48 for 20 bands" holds only if the option is set differently). With about 7 new vectors per step on average the basis grows from 20 to 120 over roughly 14 steps; the 14 restarts recorded for si8 in `davidson-work-accounting.md` are consistent. The time-average basis size is then about 70 `[E]`, against about 30 in QE (capacity 40, restart on almost every second step).
- One BAANDI step: (1) H applied to the new columns (cached for the rest); (2) incremental reduced H: new rows against all columns and old rows against new columns, two rectangles (P14B), `k (q + p)` multiply-adds times `N`; (3) full Hermitian EVD of the `q x q` matrix by faer (all `q` eigenpairs, then truncated to the band count); (4) the Ritz vectors and the H-products of ALL `m` roots by two block products, `2 N q m`; (5) residual vectors and norms of ALL roots, `O(N m)`; (6) selection of unconverged roots, preconditioner, two projection passes of the `k` corrections against the whole basis (`4 N p k`) plus an internal two-pass scalar Gram-Schmidt (about `2 N k^2`, scalar and sequential); (7) append and H-apply the new columns.
- Per-step multiply-add count `[E]`, in units of `N`: `A + B + C` with `A = k (2q - k)`, `B = 2 q m`, `C = 4 (q - k) k + 2 k^2`. QE: `4 q k`.

### 6.2 Numbers for si8 (`N = 2969`, `m = 20`) `[E]`
Use the relayed means: BAANDI `k ≈ 7` per step (2070 vectors over 290 steps), `q ≈ 70`; QE `k ≈ 9`, `q ≈ 30`.
- BAANDI per step: `A ≈ 930`, `B ≈ 2800`, `C ≈ 1860`; total about 5600 `N` multiply-adds. Over 340 steps: `5600 * 2969 * 340 = 5.7e9` complex multiply-adds, which in the 2mnk convention is `1.1e10`. The recorded explicit GEMM total after P14B is `9.49e9`; agreement within 20 percent, so `q ≈ 70` and `k ≈ 7` are plausible.
- QE per step: `4 * 30 * 9 ≈ 1100` `N`. Over 83 steps and counted the same way: about `5e8`. So QE performs on the order of 15 to 20 times fewer subspace flops for the whole run `[E]`, of which a factor of about 4 to 5 is cost per step (no all-root rotation, no orthonormalization, one rectangle instead of two) and the rest is the step count ratio 340/83.
- The `B` term (Ritz vectors and products of all roots) is half of BAANDI's step cost at `k/m = 0.35` and gets worse as `k` falls in the tail: at `k = 2` and `q = 70` the term `B` is 2800 while `A + C` is about 600, so the tail steps are dominated by work for roots that are already converged.
- H applications `[E]`: QE's FFT call count (2138) halves to at most 1069 H vectors (it also contains density and potential transforms, so this is an upper bound); BAANDI applied 2416 vectors (certificate columns included), which is 2.3 times more H vectors (an upper-bound ratio, because of the density transforms in the QE count), but 3.5 times more steps. So BAANDI works in narrower and more numerous steps; every step carries fixed all-root overhead (B, residual norms, EVD of the large basis, the restart rotations).
- Time split. QE: of 0.47 s in the band driver, 0.36 s is the Hamiltonian application (77 percent) and about 0.11 s the whole subspace machinery including the 97 small solves (0.03 s). BAANDI (Mac, T=8, P10L): of 1.30 s Davidson wall the Hamiltonian is 0.31 s (24 percent), Gram 0.29, projection 0.26 (the part of it in the scalar internal MGS is the least parallel), EVD 0.14 (sequential), paired rotation 0.14 and single rotation (see P10L table). The dev08 T=1 profile relayed in P10M: block multiplication kernels 1.0 to 1.5 s, scalar `subtract_projection` 0.28 to 1.67 s (depending on the revision), memcpy 0.17 to 0.66 s of 6.3 to 6.6 s wall. Different machines and thread counts; read only as the order of magnitude: BAANDI spends most of its Davidson time outside the Hamiltonian application, QE spends a quarter.
- EVD. 410 small solves cost 0.14 s: 0.34 ms each at average `q ≈ 70`. QE spends 0.03 s on 97 solves at `q ≈ 30`: 0.3 ms each. The EVD is about 4 percent of T=1 SCF time and 11 percent of T=8 Davidson time (sequential, Amdahl). It is not the primary lever; the cubic dependence on `q` makes it a second-order beneficiary of anything that shrinks the active basis.
- QE's range-selected path does `O(n^2 m)` for the eigenvector part. faer's self-adjoint EVD computes all `n` eigenvectors. For `n = 70`, `m = 20` the eigenvector-related part of the work is about 3 times smaller in the range-selected path, the tridiagonalization the same; `[E]` total saving 30 to 40 percent of the EVD, about 1 to 4 percent of wall. Not worth a hand-written LAPACK-style path alone (ADR 0003: no hand-written numerics for what a library provides); it matters only if a library offers selected eigenpairs with guaranteed orthogonality (inverse iteration does not guarantee it in clusters, and silicon has exact degeneracies).

### 6.3 Why BAANDI takes more steps (what can and cannot be concluded)
BAANDI needs 9 to 54 Davidson steps in every SCF iteration; QE needs 1 to 2 in about half of its SCF iterations and 11 to 20 only right after its (non-increasing) threshold tightens. Two causes are candidates and the reading cannot separate them:
1. Criterion strength. QE tests the change of the Ritz value, a quantity quadratically smaller than the residual norm: an eigenvalue change of `ethr` corresponds to a residual of about `sqrt(ethr * g)` with `g` an effective gap of order 0.1 to 1 in the same energy units. At its 14th SCF iteration QE's threshold is `5.9e-11`, a residual of order `1e-5`; BAANDI's tolerance in that iteration is `0.1` times the previous density residual, `2e-8` Ha. In the late SCF iterations BAANDI's criterion may therefore be of order 100 to 1000 times tighter in residual norm `[E]`, which at a convergence factor of 0.3 to 0.5 per step is 4 to 8 extra steps per SCF iteration. This is part of the gap but H1 (note 1) and P14A (uniform loosening) both failed to convert it into a gain, because the SCF loop in BAANDI reacted to looser vectors with more SCF iterations. That asymmetry (QE tolerates its looseness, BAANDI's SCF does not) is itself unexplained.
2. Convergence rate per step. QE restarts early and appends all unconverged roots at each step; BAANDI keeps a larger memory (capacity 120). A large memory normally helps rate, so the per-step rate of BAANDI should not be worse; but its orthonormalization against a long basis can drop near-dependent corrections silently, in which case random fallback vectors are appended (P14A: "rank loss and random fallback are unattributed"), and the tail roots (the empty buffer bands) converge by their own slow gap-limited rate.
A frozen-Hamiltonian replay (hypothesis 1) separates the two at no risk to the code base.

## 7. What the earlier BAANDI attempts say, and what is different now
- H1 (Ritz-value change with strict refinement, P7P9, rejected): tested as a replacement of the stopping rule inside the orthonormalized-basis solver. More solver iterations and slower. It leaves open the weaker, cost-oriented use of the same idea: use the small problem only to decide WHICH roots need work (the cheap QE use), keep the certificate. P14B stated the guaranteed removable bound for unlocked roots as zero; that bound relies on the stopping algorithm needing all residuals every step. The QE structure (decision from the small problem) is exactly what removes that dependence for the work term, not for the stopping guarantee.
- H3b (smaller capacity 2x, 3x, 4x, rejected, 452 versus 373 H applications at capacity 2x for 8 bands on the Gamma case): memory in the subspace pays when every root must meet a strict residual. This stands. What is different now: capacity multipliers were varied with all roots always rotated and orthonormalized, so smaller capacity only increased the number of steps. If locking and non-orthonormal products remove the per-step terms that scale with the number of locked roots and with `p`, the optimum capacity may move; test it only after those changes (hypothesis 6).
- H4 (non-orthonormal generalized formulation), never attempted: it is the one structural item of QE's step that BAANDI has not tried. The P10M CholeskyQR2 attempt replaced the MGS inside the same orthonormalized formulation; its failure (iteration counts 233 to 329 or 311 on the 20-atom skewed synthetic) shows that this system is sensitive to what the factorization does to nearly dependent corrections, but CholeskyQR2 of a full-rank block gives the same Q as MGS up to rounding (QR is unique up to phases), so the regression is a consequence of rounding amplification or of a different rank decision at the pivot test, not of the mathematical method; no record establishes which. The generalized formulation does not make that rank decision at the vector level at all; it makes it in the small problem through the canonical orthogonalization described in 1.3.
- P14A (uniform tolerance loosening fails; fixed factors 3 to 100 raise the SCF count on every system tried): applies to the SCF-level threshold, not to the per-step cost of the solver at fixed threshold. All solver-level hypotheses below keep the thresholds unchanged.
- P14B (incremental Gram, 56 percent fewer explicit GEMM flops on si8): done and kept. This note shows that QE needs only ONE rectangle for the same increment, which would remove about half of the remainder of that term, at the cost of the independent Hermiticity check (hypothesis 5).
- P10A hybrid RMM (no end-to-end crossover from 8 to 128 bands): BAANDI's RMM runs each solve to a strict residual with up to 80 sweeps and a stagnation test. QE's RMM does one sweep of four steps per SCF iteration and does not converge. These are different regimes; the benchmark does not say anything about the single-sweep regime, but that regime cannot certify intermediate maps (hypothesis 7).
- CholeskyQR2 vs orthonormalization cost: the scalar internal MGS is 4 to 26 percent of dev08 T=1 wall depending on the revision, so removing it matters; but "removing it" by a different factorization was rejected, which is why the structural alternative (not orthonormalizing at all) is the clean route.

## Hypotheses for BAANDI

Ordering is by expected value divided by risk. Every hypothesis keeps the thresholds, the certificate and the SCF policy unchanged, uses the existing accounting (P14A/P14B counters) as its measurement tool, and is judged on the ledger's benchmark set (si8, k444s, the synthetic 20-atom skewed, 4-atom elongated, 12-Bohr cubic, and the 19-case suite) so that nothing is adopted on silicon alone.

1. **Frozen-Hamiltonian replay to separate criterion strength from convergence rate** (measurement, no code change to the default).
   Idea: take the Hamiltonian and the starting vectors of one mid and one late SCF iteration of si8 and of two other systems; run BAANDI's Davidson with (a) its current rule, (b) the QE rule (Ritz-value change) as a diagnostic only, and log per root and per step the residual norm and the Ritz-value change. Record the steps at which (b) would have stopped and the residual norms there.
   Expected gain: none directly; it decides where hypotheses 2 to 4 are worth their risk. If (b) stops at residuals around `1e-5` while (a) needs `1e-8`, the gap is criterion strength and the remaining lever is the SCF coupling (not the solver); if the rates per step differ, the lever is the solver.
   Risk: none. Validation gate: only the counters. Conflicts: none; consistent with H1/H6 outcomes.

2. **Hard locking (deflation) of converged roots with a safety margin and an all-root final rotation.**
   Idea: when a root's residual is below `tol/c` (`c` about `sqrt(m)`, the loss when the final Rayleigh-Ritz mixes locked vectors) AND the root does not belong to a cluster whose other members are not locked (lock the lowest roots first, whole clusters only), freeze its vector and its H-product; the reduced problem and its EVD run over the active vectors only (`q - locked`); new corrections are orthogonalized against locked and active vectors; at the end one rotation of all `m` vectors and the existing certificate on all roots. Unlock automatically if the certificate fails.
   Expected gain `[E]`: removes most of `B` (it falls from `2 q m` to about `2 q_active k`), shrinks `q` in `A`, `C` and the EVD cubically. With `k/m ≈ 0.35` and a locked fraction of about 65 percent over the run: 30 to 40 percent of subspace multiply-adds and 40 to 60 percent of EVD time (sequential part), i.e. perhaps 10 to 20 percent of SCF wall at T=1 and more at T=8 where the sequential parts bound scaling. Basis: P14B says the zero safe-removable bound holds only for unlocked roots and calls the full site cost the ceiling for a locking algorithm; the cost model in 6.2.
   Risk: a locked vector can later be degraded by cluster rotation (mitigated by the cluster rule and by the margin), a changed trajectory (P14B toys changed counts by rounding; P14A shows the SCF is sensitive), more total steps if the margin is too large.
   Validation gate: energy within 1e-12 Ha and SCF count unchanged on the four realistic fixtures; all 16 converged suite cases still converge within the suite energy tolerance (counts may change on toys, to be reported); all certificates pass without failures; H vectors not above baseline by more than 2 percent; the P14B counters show at least a 25 percent reduction of subspace multiply-adds on si8 and k444s.
   Conflicts: none with the ledger; it supersedes the "unlocked" assumption behind P14B's zero bound, and must be reported as a new algorithm, not as an optimization of the old one.

3. **Non-orthonormal basis with a conditioned small generalized problem (H4 revisited).**
   Idea: normalize corrections to unit length only; build the Gram rectangle (`N q k`) in addition to the H rectangle; solve `H_red c = e S_red c` by canonical orthogonalization (Hermitian EVD of `S_red`, discard directions below a relative threshold, whiten, second EVD) with a Cholesky fast path that is checked by a pivot test and falls back to the canonical path; contract and restart as now. Orthonormalize only on restart (the Ritz vectors are the restart basis; whether they are exactly orthonormal after canonical whitening is then a testable property).
   Expected gain `[E]`: removes `C` (about one third of the multiply-adds, plus the scalar sequential MGS, which is 4 to 26 percent of dev08 T=1 wall), adds about 5 percent for the Gram rectangle; net 25 to 30 percent of subspace work and the main serial bottleneck in the threaded runs.
   Risk: cond(`S_red`) enters the accuracy: eigenvalue/vector accuracy of order `eps * cond`; the tolerance floor 1e-9 needs cond below about 1e6, which a basis restarted every 14 steps and normalized per column usually keeps, but nearly dependent corrections are exactly the 20-atom skew case where CholeskyQR2 regressed. A stage-0 diagnostic is mandatory: count, on that fixture, the retained rank per step under MGS and under CholeskyQR2 and the smallest eigenvalue of the Gram matrix, to find out whether the earlier regression was a rank decision or rounding.
   Validation gate: same as hypothesis 2, plus the minimum eigenvalue of `S_red` logged on every case, no case with cond above 1e8 without a triggered fallback, and the small solve's reported Hermiticity/orthogonality defect unchanged.
   Conflicts: contradicts nothing recorded (H4 not attempted); the P10M CholeskyQR2 rejection is evidence of sensitivity on the skew case and sets the first test. Interacts with hypothesis 2 (both change the structure of the step; do them in the order 2 then 3, or 3 then 2 with the same fixtures, never together).

4. **Thick restart with previous directions (GD+k style, literature principle, not QE).**
   Idea: when the basis restarts, keep, besides the `m` Ritz vectors, the previous-step Ritz vectors (or their differences) of the still-unconverged roots, with their H-products from the stored data (no extra H application). This preserves CG-like memory across restarts for `k` extra columns.
   Expected gain `[E]`: unknown without measurement; the evidence in H3b is that memory in the subspace reduces the number of applications (capacity 2x gave 452 versus 373 applications), and restarts happen every 14 steps in si8, each discarding that memory. If tail steps (where `k` is small) recover 20 to 30 percent faster convergence, steps drop proportionally and the per-step overhead of hypothesis 2 shrinks further.
   Risk: dependent columns (previous and current Ritz vectors nearly parallel in the tail) increase rank loss; adds `k` columns to every restart.
   Validation gate: step and H-vector counts reduced on at least three of the four realistic fixtures and not increased on any; energy unchanged.
   Conflicts: none; opposite of H3b's "smaller memory" direction, so it should not be combined with smaller capacity until measured.

5. **One rectangle for the reduced H, Hermitian fill by conjugation (QE style).**
   Idea: compute only the new rows against all columns; obtain the old rows against new columns by conjugation; force real diagonal. Keep the independent Hermiticity check by testing it once per solve on a sample or in debug and certificate passes, not on every step.
   Expected gain `[E]`: about 40 to 45 percent of term `A`, i.e. 6 to 8 percent of the subspace multiply-adds; small but nearly free.
   Risk: negligible numerically (the matrix is exactly Hermitian up to rounding by construction); the P14B design chose the two-rectangle form deliberately to keep a genuine check.
   Validation gate: energies within 1e-12 Ha, bit-compatible within the adopted pure-Rust backend at T=1,2,4,8, the Hermitian defect reported on the certificate pass.
   Conflicts: reverses the explicit P14B statement "no triangle is inferred by conjugation"; record that decision and its reason.

6. **Capacity and restart policy retuned after hypotheses 2 to 4.**
   Idea: define capacity in terms of active vectors; sweep multipliers 2, 3, 4, 6 on the whole benchmark set once locking is in.
   Expected gain `[E]`: unknown; the EVD and `A`, `C` terms grow with `q`, so a smaller `q` pays only if steps do not increase. H3b says it did not pay before.
   Risk: low, parameter change; default changes need the generality rule (no tuning on silicon alone).
   Validation gate: no default changes unless every case in the set is not worse and the aggregate is better.
   Conflicts: H3b; only meaningful after 2 to 4.

7. **Single-sweep RMM in the SCF loop with Davidson on the first iterations, every fifth iteration and the final certified iteration (QE regime), as a switchable module.**
   Idea: reuse the existing RMM module as the QE hybrid: warm-up Davidson at SCF iterations 1 and 2, RMM sweep of fixed depth (4 steps) otherwise, Davidson with certificate on the final iteration. Its cost per iteration is about `5 m` H vectors and `O(N m^2)` projection work, with no growing subspace.
   Expected gain `[E]`: removes the Davidson subspace machinery in intermediate iterations; versus BAANDI's current roughly 160 vectors per SCF iteration at `m = 20`, about 100, plus the subspace work: potentially 30 to 40 percent of SCF wall for the intermediate iterations IF the SCF count does not increase; QE reports it as an option rather than a default, and BAANDI's P10A found no end-to-end crossover for the converged variant.
   Risk: intermediate maps are uncertified; the SCF count may rise (the sensitivity seen in P14A suggests this); sweeps that do not converge hide trouble until the final Davidson.
   Validation gate: the ledger rule for any default change: SCF count within one iteration and energy within 1e-12 Ha on all suite cases at identical final certificates; default unchanged unless it wins on every system class (cubic, skewed, elongated, slab; small and large band counts; small and large gap).
   Conflicts: P10A's negative result is for the converged variant; the sweep variant is untested, so no conflict, but the generality rule makes a default change unlikely.

8. **Atomic-pseudo-wavefunction starting vectors with an initial range-selected rotation** (QE start), instead of the free-electron start.
   Idea: build starting vectors from the UPF pseudo-atomic wavefunctions where present (fallback to the current start), rotate once.
   Expected gain `[E]`: the first SCF iteration is 9 steps and 104 applications of 2070 (5 percent) for si8; halving it gains about 2 to 3 percent of H work plus possibly a better first density.
   Risk: low; the UPF may lack wavefunctions (synthetic fixtures), so it is optional.
   Validation gate: first-iteration steps reduced, final energy unchanged on all fixtures; nothing committed from UPF files.
   Conflicts: none.

Not recommended: ParO as a replacement (no reduction of H applications; its advantage is parallel structure), the real-wavefunction Gamma trick for the complex Gamma case (separate topic, QE's relayed run used complex Gamma on purpose), band-by-band CG (slower by design), hand-written range-selected dense solver (library policy, and orthogonality in degenerate clusters is a risk), per-band gap-scaled thresholds (needs a density-error model; the current strict-plus-buffer scheme already covers the empty-band lever that QE has).
