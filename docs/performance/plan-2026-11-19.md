# BAANDI performance review and next experiments · 19 November 2026

**Decision for the implementation team.** Keep the distributed grid, mixer and owned-field defaults. The largest new research opportunity is to use orbital information already produced by one expensive fine solve to predict several density updates cheaply before the next full solve. First test whether that information is sufficient on a held-out trajectory. In parallel, implement the smaller, directly testable change in §3 that removes duplicate FFTs from the experimental Coulomb mixing guard. Keep the current certification contract.

Reviewed main `63aa4adb`, 40 commits past the previous review at `6be8f095`. The repository records a later user decision to pursue **one core and pure MPI**. New throughput claims must compare the same model on both sides. The target remains verified launch-to-exit BAANDI wall ≤0.90 times the fastest eligible configuration per cell. QE is the measured comparator in this review. This review used published code, relayed dev08 records and a small independent algebra check. No dev08 jobs were run here. ## 3. First implementation — give the mixer the residual spectrum it already paid for

**Finding.** On the owned M2 route `GridPlan::stop_error` already reads both distributed spectra and forms their difference. When the Coulomb guard is enabled, `DistributedPulay::coulomb_guard` creates a real trial residual and its own plan transforms **two fields per check**. A refit transforms **m+1 additional fields** for m retained differences. Each transform moves data between ranks. This is duplication across the stop and mixer stages. The previous suggestion to reuse the next mixed density targeted a different FFT. [Existing spectra and stop metric][grid] · [Guard and counters][mix]

**Proposal.** On eligible maps export an epoch-tagged read-only residual spectrum, r̂ = ρ̂out − ρ̂in. Keep the last m+1 *actual* residual spectra on the owning ranks. Maintain their small Coulomb Gram matrix K with entries ⟨rᵢ,rⱼ⟩C, using the exact current weights, normalization and canonical reductions. One new residual needs only its new row and diagonal. For Pulay's trial p = r − Σ γⱼΔrⱼ, form its coefficient vector a over the raw residual history. Then ⟨p,p⟩C = aᵀKa. The difference Gram and right-hand side for a refit follow from the same K by taking row/column differences. This first experiment changes how the **existing** guard is evaluated. It introduces no new damping rule.

I checked this identity independently with 7 real synthetic residual fields on an odd 7×9×5 periodic grid, integer-mode Coulomb weights, G=0 excluded, and 100 coefficient vectors. The maximum relative discrepancy from explicitly Fourier-transforming the trial field was **6.9e−16** in float64. This is algebra validation only. Production normalization, Nyquist cases, determinism and wall time still need testing.

**Preregister three arms.** A = current default guard off. B = current guard on. C = same guard with a proposed default-off `BAANDI_M2_GUARD_GRAM`. B→C prices the shortcut. A→C decides if the guard earns its place in production. Count transforms, plan builds, reductions, extra history bytes, check/refit decisions, full H columns, verification retries and end-to-end seconds. With h eligible checks and refits of widths mⱼ, at most **2h + Σ(mⱼ+1)** guard transforms disappear. Credit zero seconds against default A until A→C wins. Subtract history streaming and any added collective. Keep the direct path on clamp, stale epoch, unsupported layout, history reseed, or an ambiguous decision at the rounding boundary. Recompute near-zero quadratic forms directly when cancellation dominates. Verify rank-count determinism and decisions on odd/even, skewed and ill-conditioned histories. Stage 1 prices the work. Full SCF decides the trajectory gain.

## 4. Biggest upside — predict the next fine density with a small orbital problem

**Hypothesis.** Several consecutive fine solves may spend most of their time rediscovering a density response already represented in the **last full solve's occupied orbitals and a few complementary directions**. The new evidence is the large absolute ordinary-fine budget and the observed mixer overshoot with accurate inner solves. Davidson's lean workspace already forms selected residual/correction blocks, rotates basis vectors and retains H products during a solve. Test whether those vectors contain the response *before* building a new solver. [Lean workspace][lean] · [SCF trajectory study][traj]

**Smallest numerical experiment.** At the first expensive fine map after handoff construct an orthonormal basis Y from its occupied orbitals, available nearby empty Ritz vectors, and a few linearly independent recent correction directions. Record an occupied-only negative control: a unitary rotation within a fully occupied, equally occupied space leaves density unchanged. Empty or complementary directions are essential. Use only information available at that map to build Y. Hold later exact densities/orbitals aside for evaluation. For initial scope use insulating Γ-point, fixed occupations. Flag near gap closures, incomplete clusters, new occupied states and rank loss as fallback cases.

For a candidate density ρ near the captured one, project the fine Hamiltonian as

```text
H_Y(ρ) = Y† H(ρ₀) Y + Y† [V_Hxc(ρ) − V_Hxc(ρ₀)] Y.
```

This identity holds in the fixed subspace for a fixed cell, fixed density-independent nonlocal pseudopotential and local density-dependent KS potential. Use the full fine-grid quadrature, including correct Γ representation. Extend to other operator classes only after separately deriving the valid update. Diagonalize this small matrix, reconstruct the occupied density, and try one or two density updates **as a predictor**. Resume the ordinary full-basis map at the proposed density and perform the original convergence and fixed-point checks. Only exact production-map outputs enter the ordinary Pulay history. No projected solution is delivered.

**Falsify cheaply.** On the same saved pre-step state compare (a) current Pulay, (b) the current guard and (c) the projected predictor. Measure the reduction of the *actual next full-map* Coulomb residual and total remaining verified time. Also report the occupied-subspace principal angles to later exact solutions and the predictor's low-G density error. Forbid future orbitals in the predictor. Restrict candidate direction counts on training fixtures, freeze the choice, and report held-out structures. Persisting or rotating full grids for Y, contracting Y†ΔVY (potentially O(Ngrid × dim(Y)²)), distributed reductions and reconstructing density can all erase a win. Price them with the actual Si64 dimensions before optimizing the model. No need to store a grid field for every orbital pair.

**Why this deserves a different experiment from Advance.** BT3 limited the corrections used to evaluate each ordinary map. In two Si16 cases the resulting path paid 37–38% more H work. Here several cheap evaluations stay inside one predictor, followed by a full map, and never supply approximate residual history to the production solver. The benefit has to be **entire expensive maps removed**. Count every rejected trial and extra full solve. If one predictor replaces k full maps, its net return is the actual cost of those k maps minus all predictor and downstream costs. Repeated exact corrections or weak held-out prediction close the experiment. A positive frozen-H timing alone cannot promote it. Finite-element multilevel correction work motivates the separation of a small nonlinear problem and full-space corrections. Its published timings do not predict this plane-wave adaptation. [BT3 design and ledger][advance] · [Hu, Xie and Xu, 2018][paper1] · [Their 2023 follow-up][paper2]

## 5. A second way to reduce outside time — keep orbitals distributed across cutoffs

The default owned-density work ends at an important boundary. The ladder's coarse stage calls `warm_state()` or explicitly materializes the terminal lazy block. `CoarseState` carries a full complex orbital matrix. `prolong_orbitals` allocates a fine-basis matrix of zeros and copies entries by Miller index. The new fine stage then consumes that state. These operations are mathematically an injection of a smaller index set into a larger one. They do not intrinsically require every rank to hold every coefficient. The earlier LADDER-IMPORT-SPLIT distributed validation, and LAZY-ASSEMBLE-FUSED sped up materialization. Neither removed this interface. [Coarse return][handoff] · [Prolongation][embed] · [Ledger][ledger]

**Experiment.** Price just the handoff on current Si64 MPI8 first. Prototype a direct coarse-owner → fine-owner transfer by integer Miller index. Precompute the permutation once for this pair of layouts, send only populated rows and fill the remaining owned fine rows with zero. Check conjugate partners, Γ packing factors, the zero mode, differing ownership, and identical complex-to-real conventions. Keep the full-matrix route as an oracle and for unsupported consumers. Preserve import norm/orthogonality checks, checkpoint behavior and potential identity. A pure copy/permutation in identical storage can be bit-identical, while repacking or a different reduction order requires rounding-class validation. Record peak bytes/rank, bytes transmitted, collective calls, gather/materialization count and net handoff seconds. Its ceiling is the measured exclusive handoff cost, **never the full 1.2007 s coarse solve/stage**.

A higher-risk extension is to prepare independent fine-grid tables on some of the allocated ranks while the other ranks execute the coarse SCF. Screen the economics on the same eight physical cores first. Compare serial duration Tfine-prep(8) + Tcoarse(8) with max(Tfine-prep(8−p), Tcoarse(p)) + redistribution/join/remaining work. Shared geometry and PP preparation, non-overlappable initialization, memory contention and communicator overhead belong in both models. The current MPI handle binds to WORLD. A production fork/join would need explicit communicator ownership across calls and agreement on errors. No generic asynchronous collective rewrite is warranted unless the time budget survives this screen. Coordinate with F-INIT-STATES. [Coarse constructor][construct] · [MPI communicator][mpiwrap]
