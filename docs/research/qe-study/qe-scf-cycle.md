## 1. What one SCF iteration does in QE (norm-conserving, LDA, no symmetry-related extras)

Ordered list, with the work each step implies. Units: QE works in Rydberg; every energy below is quoted in Ry with the Hartree value in parentheses when it matters (1 Ha = 2 Ry).

1. Set the diagonalization threshold ethr from the previous iteration's error estimate (section 5.2).
2. Compute `deband_hwf`-type bookkeeping from the old density and old potential: one dot product of density and Hartree+xc potential on the grid. No transform.
3. Diagonalize at every k point (block Davidson, covered in note 1). The starting vectors are the previous iteration's vectors; only at iteration 1 are they the Rayleigh-Ritz-rotated starting set (section 2.3).
4. Accumulate the output density (section 3): one inverse FFT per band (per pair of bands in real Gamma mode), then one forward FFT of the summed density, optional symmetrization in G space, one inverse FFT back to real space.
5. Compute `deband` = minus the integral of the output density times the OLD Hartree+xc potential (one dot product).
6. Mix (section 4). Cost: dot products over the density sphere in G space, a very small dense solve, and, when the smooth and dense G sets coincide, one inverse FFT to regenerate the real-space mixed density.
7. If the first iteration shows that the threshold was too loose, redo steps 3 to 6 once with a tighter threshold (section 5.3).
8. If not converged: compute the new Hartree and xc potential from the mixed density (section 3.2), compute `descf` (section 6), copy the mixed density into the working density. If converged: recompute the potential from the OUTPUT density, store the difference to the previous one for forces, and set `descf` to zero.
9. Assemble the total energy and print the estimated SCF accuracy and the Harris-Foulkes estimate.

The relayed dev08 timing for si8 (14 SCF iterations, one process) shows that the non-eigensolver part is not negligible once the eigensolver is fast: c_bands 0.47 s, the rest of the SCF cycle 0.14 s (sum_band 0.07, v_of_rho 0.03, mix_rho 0.04) and init_run 0.05 s of a 0.68 s run. That is about 20 percent outside the eigensolver and 7 percent in setup. In BAANDI the same phases are 0.1 to 0.2 s of 5 to 6 s (2 to 4 percent), so they do not matter today, but they would be 25 to 40 percent of a run at QE speed. They must be brought to roughly the QE level (a few milliseconds per iteration for si8) when the eigensolver reaches parity.

## 2. Setup and initialization

### 2.1 Order and cost model
`init_run` builds, in this order: G vectors for the dense set and for the smooth set, the G shells (groups of G vectors with equal |G|, used so that every radial quantity is computed once per shell), the local-potential and core-charge tables per shell, the symmetrization tables, the nonlocal projector tables (`init_us_1`), the Hamiltonian-independent kinetic data, then the starting potential (`potinit`), the screened-coefficient tables for the nonlocal term (`newd`, trivial for norm-conserving) and the starting wavefunctions (`wfcinit`). The whole block costs 0.05 s on si8 (7 percent of a 0.68 s run), so it is a one-off cost that scales with the number of G vectors and atoms, not with SCF iterations.

The ideas worth knowing:
- Everything radial is evaluated per G shell, not per G vector. In a cell with high symmetry the number of shells is a small fraction of the number of G vectors. BAANDI already does this for the core density (a map keyed by |G|^2) and should check that the local potential, the Hartree kernel and the beta transforms use the same trick wherever shells repeat; for k-point sets with generic k the shell count approaches the vector count and the trick gives nothing.
- The nonlocal projector radial transforms are tabulated once on a uniform grid in |q| with spacing 0.01 Bohr^-1 and interpolated with four-point Lagrange interpolation at each k+G, so the per-k setup costs an interpolation per G and projector instead of a radial quadrature. That table is the dominant reason the per-k setup is cheap in QE. It trades accuracy (interpolation error, small at this spacing) for speed. For BAANDI the equivalent is a measurement question: compare the per-k projector setup time with a direct quadrature per distinct |k+G| on the real meshes (1200 to 1800 points) at the k444s and larger-cell cases before building any table. A table would change numbers at the 1e-9 level and so cannot be adopted under the "energy within 1e-12 Ha" rule without a precise accuracy study; direct quadrature on distinct shells is bit-stable.
- `c_bands` and `sum_band` both rebuild the projector values for the current k point in every SCF iteration (they are not cached across iterations when more than one k point exists, to save memory). For a Gamma-only run BAANDI can simply keep them; for many k points it is a memory-versus-time choice that should be measured, since the per-k rebuild in QE is part of the 0.14 s above.

### 2.2 Starting density (atomic charge superposition)
`potinit` builds the starting density in reciprocal space as the sum over atomic species of the radial valence charge stored in the pseudopotential file (the atomic valence density tabulated on the radial mesh, one of the standard sections of UPF v2) transformed to |G| per shell, multiplied by the structure factor of the species. The transform is done through a table in |G| built once. The density is then renormalized so that its G=0 component equals N_electrons/Omega (a uniform charge is used if the starting charge is essentially zero). A warning is printed when the real-space density has a total negative part beyond 1e-4 electrons, which can occur from truncation ringing at the density cutoff; QE does not forbid it and its xc routines use the absolute value of the density with a small threshold.

Why it matters: the starting potential is built from this density, the first diagonalization and the first mixing step start from a physically reasonable charge, and QE needs 14 iterations for si8 from this start (est. accuracy at iteration 1: 0.436 Ry). BAANDI starts from the uniform density (`uniform_density`), which is the worst possible start for a covalent crystal and for any system with localized charge; the first residual (0.026 normalized) must be removed by mixing. The UPF parser in `crates/dft-upf` currently skips the atomic-density and the pseudo-wavefunction sections, so neither start is available yet.

### 2.3 Starting wavefunctions and the initial Rayleigh-Ritz rotation
`wfcinit`/`init_wfc` build, per k point, a set of starting vectors of size max(number of atomic pseudo-wavefunctions, number of bands): the atomic pseudo-wavefunctions of the pseudopotential file (radial function times a real spherical harmonic times the structure factor, evaluated at k+G through the same kind of radial table), each multiplied by a small random complex perturbation of relative size 5 percent ("atomic+random"), and, if bands exceed atomic functions, extra bands from random complex numbers with magnitude decaying as 1/((k+G)^2+1). A Rayleigh-Ritz rotation (`rotate_wfc`, dense generalized eigenproblem of dimension max(natomwfc, nbnd), then keep the lowest nbnd) in this starting space produces the first eigenvector set and eigenvalues. Cost: one H application on 32 vectors for si8 (4 atomic functions per atom, 8 atoms) plus a 32 by 32 dense problem per k point: comparable to two or three Davidson steps. This happens once, before the first iteration; in the first diagonalization Davidson is told that its starting vectors are already Ritz vectors, so it skips the initial reduced-matrix build (the later iterations build it from the old vectors against the new potential).

Effect in the data: QE reports an average of 3.0 and 4.0 Davidson iterations in SCF iterations 1 and 2 on si8 with ethr 1e-2 and 1.4e-3; BAANDI needs 9 and 10 steps at tolerances 1e-2 and 2.6e-3 (residual-norm units) starting from the lowest-kinetic plane waves. The difference is partly the criterion (section 5.4) and partly the quality of the start; this note cannot separate them (experiment E8).

## 3. Density accumulation, potential construction

### 3.1 `sum_band` (density)
1. Band weights are computed from the occupations (fixed occupations for insulators; with smearing the Fermi level is found first). The band-type flag for the eigensolver (strict versus loose for nearly empty bands) is also set here: a band is "empty" when its occupation is below 1 percent of the maximum. For insulators with the default nbnd = nelec/2 there are no empty bands.
2. For every k point, the vectors are read from memory (or from the buffer when there are several k points), the projector table is rebuilt only for ultrasoft, and bands are transformed to the smooth real-space grid. In the Gamma-only (real wavefunction) mode two bands are put through one complex FFT (one in the real part and one in the imaginary part), and the density increment is the weighted sum of the squares of the real and the imaginary parts: one FFT per two bands. In the complex mode (K_POINTS automatic 1 1 1 as in the relayed comparison) it is one FFT per band. The weight factor includes the 1/Omega normalization.
3. After the k and band loops (and the sum over pools), the density is transformed to G space on the smooth grid, ultrasoft augmentation is added (not needed), and the density is symmetrized in G space (the group average over crystal symmetries, applied only in the irreducible-wedge k-point mode).
4. The symmetrized density is transformed back to real space on the dense grid.

Consequences: for NC pseudopotentials with the default cutoff ratio 4 the smooth and dense grids are identical, so no interpolation occurs. The interpolation machinery in FFTXlib (smooth to dense, dense to smooth) is used only when the density cutoff exceeds four times the wavefunction cutoff (ultrasoft and PAW, or a user-raised ecutrho). For BAANDI this would matter only if a future system wants an ecutrho above 4 Ecut, for example very hard norm-conserving core charges; then the wavefunction products can still be done on the 4 Ecut grid, and the potential applied on the finer grid by Fourier interpolation (zero padding in G space), which is exact for a band-limited function.

Two structural points are more important than the details:
- The real-wavefunction Gamma mode halves the FFT count in both the density and, more importantly, the H application, and halves the vector length (half sphere). The relayed comparison deliberately used QE's complex Gamma mode for equal footing. The target "exceed QE against its best configuration" (AGENTS.md, performance target) means that for Gamma-only systems QE's best configuration is the real mode, which is roughly twice as cheap as what was compared. This is outside the area of this note but it changes what "QE speed" is for Gamma runs.
- By default QE reduces k points by crystal symmetry and symmetrizes the density in G space. The reference runs in this repository use `nosym` (so only time reversal reduces the set, `docs/validation.md`). That is a legitimate equal-footing choice, but for the multi-k "QE best configuration" comparison the default (symmetry on) is the speed to beat and can be much cheaper than the 32 k points relayed for k444s.

### 3.2 `v_of_rho` (potential)
- Hartree is computed entirely in G space: the density is already available in G space from `sum_band`/`mix_rho`, each coefficient is multiplied by 4 pi e^2/G^2 (G=0 dropped), the energy is the sum of coefficient times conjugate, and a single inverse FFT returns the potential. One FFT, no forward transform, loops only over the density sphere, and in Gamma mode only over half the sphere.
- LDA xc is a pointwise loop in real space on the sum of valence and core density; there is no FFT. The energy and the potential integral are accumulated in the same loop. The negative-density total is reported but not corrected. The xc library supplies the point function.
- GGA (not used by the MVP but needed for P7B): the density is transformed to G space (one forward FFT per spin), its gradient components are formed in G space (multiply by iG) and brought back (three inverse FFTs), the point function gives the energy density and the derivatives with respect to the density and to the squared gradient, then the vector field (derivative with respect to the gradient times the gradient) is transformed (three forward FFTs), differentiated in G space and brought back with the divergence combined in G space (one inverse FFT): roughly eight dense FFTs per iteration, plus a point-function call that thresholds at 1e-6 in density and 1e-10 in gradient squared. The thresholds are numerical hygiene and each is a choice; BAANDI's `gga.rs` uses three derivative transforms forward plus inverse each and one further per flux component (about twelve full complex FFTs), so a count of FFTs is a direct comparison.
- The Hartree and xc results are added into one array of the effective local potential and then set (`set_vrs`) into the array used by `h_psi`, interpolating to the smooth grid when they differ (not here).

### 3.3 Cost comparison in FFT units (dense grid, per SCF iteration, NC LDA, one k)
QE: bands/2 or bands (density) + 1 (density to G) + 1 (symmetrized density to r) + 1 (mixed density to r) + 1 (Hartree) = bands(/2) + 4. BAANDI (code reading, not a profile): bands (density through the fused plan) + Hartree for the input density (two full complex transforms of a real field) + Hartree again for the output density inside the energy audit (two more) + the local-energy transform in the audit + the mixed-density clamp (none) = bands + about 5 full complex transforms, plus the same again in the certificate iteration. The non-density transforms are therefore about 5 versus 3 to 4; the real difference is not the count but that BAANDI transforms real fields as complex and over the whole box, not the density sphere, and rebuilds the squared-frequency array on every Hartree call.

## 4. Density mixing (`mix_rho`)

### 4.1 Algorithm
This is a modified Broyden scheme in the secant (Anderson-like) form:
1. The unknown is the density in reciprocal space (the smooth-grid sphere; plus kinetic-energy density and occupation matrices when applicable, none for MVP). The G=0 component is not an unknown: it equals N/Omega for both input and output, so the residual has no G=0 part and the combination keeps the charge exact by construction.
2. At iteration j the residual is F_j = rho_out_j - rho_in_j in G space. The scalar SCF error estimate is dr2 = <F_j, F_j> in the metric of section 4.2. If dr2 is below the user threshold (conv_thr, default 1e-6 Ry), the cycle is declared converged and the mixing step is skipped (no further density update). A second exit, taken before mixing, is when dr2 is below the estimate of the diagonalization error (only meaningful in the first iteration), see section 5.3.
3. The history holds the last mixing_ndim = 8 pairs of differences: the change in the residual (Delta F_i = F_{i+1} - F_i) and the change in the input density (Delta rho_i). The history is a ring buffer; with a full buffer the oldest pair is replaced. It is kept in memory or in a scratch file depending on io_level.
4. The normal equations of the residual least-squares problem are formed in the chosen metric: A_ik = <Delta F_i, Delta F_k>, b_i = <Delta F_i, F_j>; A is symmetric and is inverted by an indefinite-symmetric factorization without regularization (a singular A is an error). The coefficients are gamma = A^{-1} b.
5. The extrapolated input and residual are rho_in^bar = rho_in - sum_i gamma_i Delta rho_i and F^bar = F - sum_i gamma_i Delta F_i. The new input is rho_in^bar + beta F^bar with beta = mixing_beta, default 0.7 for the plain case (lower values, 0.1 to 0.2, are selected internally only for special solvation or grand-canonical modes). At the first iteration the history is empty and the update is plain linear mixing with beta.
6. The final residual F^bar can be preconditioned before the last step (modes "TF" and "local-TF"): the Thomas-Fermi mode multiplies each residual component by G^2/(G^2 + k_TF^2) with k_TF^2 derived from the average density through the electron-gas relation (the Kerker form; a coefficient proportional to the cube root of 1/r_s^2 for the Wigner-Seitz radius r_s of the average density); the local version builds a spatially varying screening by solving a small sequence of problems. The default is the plain mode (no preconditioner). The preconditioner is applied only to the last step, not to the stored history.
7. The components of the density between the smooth-grid sphere and the dense-grid sphere (empty when the cutoffs are in ratio 4) are mixed linearly with beta instead of through the Broyden history. When the two spheres coincide, this step sets the high-frequency part to zero and regenerates the real-space density from the mixed G components (one inverse FFT).
8. In 7.6 the limit of the mixed G components is the smooth-grid count on every call; there is no low-G-only restriction in the plain default case. (Older versions limited the history inner products to a low-G subset to save time; that is not the behavior to emulate.)

### 4.2 The metric and why it is Hartree-weighted
The inner product is <a, b> = (Omega/2) * 4 pi e^2 * sum over G != 0 of Re[conj(a(G)) b(G)] / |G|^2 (with the factor two for the half-sphere in Gamma mode), so <F, F> is exactly the Hartree energy of the residual density (in Ry; in Hartree units drop the factor e^2 = 2). Reasons, stated in terms of first principles:
- To second order, the error of the energy functional at a trial density rho_in around the fixed point rho* is a quadratic form in (rho_in - rho*) whose dominant positive part is the Hartree kernel; the xc and kinetic kernels are smaller and have bounded range. Hence the Hartree norm of the residual is a cheap, parameter-free estimate of the energy error of the iterate, and dr2 can be compared with an energy threshold directly (conv_thr is in Ry and is meant as an accuracy on the total energy).
- The same kernel weights the long-wavelength components of the residual most. Those are the charge-sloshing modes whose damping is poor in plain mixing; in the least-squares fit of the Broyden coefficients they dominate the Gram matrix, so the fit is steered to cancel the slow modes. High-G components, where the Hartree kernel is small, carry the noise from the inexact diagonalization and from the finite grid; the weighting suppresses their influence on the coefficients. With a plain L2 metric all components count equally, the Gram matrix is dominated by the bulk of the density's large Fourier components, and diagonalization noise in the density affects the coefficients on the same footing as the signal.
- The fit solves a small problem in the full complex G space; per iteration the dot products touch each history vector once over the density sphere, with no FFT.

### 4.3 What QE does not do (and BAANDI does)
No positivity safeguard: QE can produce a mixed density with negative samples and continues (warning only). BAANDI's convex fraction toward the extrapolated density plus a history reset on negativity (`session.rs`) is stricter and, when triggered, throws away the Pulay history, which costs iterations. The number of resets on the benchmark set is recorded per iteration (`mixing_resets`) but is not summarized anywhere in the notes read; it should be (experiment E9).

## 5. The SCF error estimate and the threshold coupling

### 5.1 dr2
dr2 is the Hartree-metric squared norm of the residual (section 4.2), computed once per iteration over the density sphere including both spin channels when present. It is the number printed as "estimated scf accuracy" and compared with conv_thr. It is not the energy error itself (it ignores xc, kinetic and the screening of the response), but it tracks the energy error within a modest factor on the insulators studied.

### 5.2 The coupling rule, and the fact that it is a ratchet
From the second iteration on, the diagonalization threshold in Ry is the minimum of its current value and 0.1 * dr2 / max(1, N_electrons), floored at 1e-13 Ry. Three observations from the relayed dev08 data (verified by reproducing the arithmetic: iteration 2 ethr 1.36e-3 equals 0.1 * 0.436 / 32; iteration 3 equals 0.1 * 0.01929 / 32; iteration 5 keeps the value of iteration 4):
- The rule is a ratchet: the threshold never increases, even when the SCF error increases (iteration 4: dr2 rose from 8.2e-4 to 1.5e-3, and the threshold stayed at the lower value).
- It uses the error estimate of the previous iteration, since the current iteration's estimate is only known after the diagonalization.
- The threshold has the meaning of an eigenvalue accuracy (energy), and 0.1 * dr2 / N reads: the total diagonalization error, summed over N/2 doubly occupied bands, should be about a tenth of the SCF error, with the electron count as the proxy for how many bands contribute.

The pattern visible in the per-iteration Davidson counts (3, 4, 4, 12, 2, 7, 2, 2, 11, 1, 11, 2, 20, 2 on si8) is explained by the ratchet and by Davidson starting from the previous vectors: an iteration whose threshold equals the previous one starts from vectors already converged to that accuracy for a slightly different potential and needs one or two steps (iterations 5, 7, 8, 10, 12, 14); an iteration where the threshold drops by a factor of three or more needs 7 to 20 steps. This is an inference from the data, not from the source.

### 5.3 First-iteration safeguard
At iteration 1 the threshold is a fixed initial value (1e-2 Ry for a starting atomic potential; 1e-5 for a potential read from file), and an estimate of the diagonalization error is formed as tr2_min = ethr * max(1, N_electrons). If after mixing the first residual has dr2 < tr2_min, the first diagonalization was too sloppy relative to the SCF error: the threshold is reset to 0.1 * dr2 / max(1,N), floored, and the first iteration is repeated (no mixing history was created). For si8 dr2 = 0.436 Ry against tr2_min = 0.32 Ry: the safeguard did not fire but the margin is only 1.4. It matters for good starting guesses (restart from a nearly converged density) where dr2 starts small.

### 5.4 Convergence test of the eigensolver, and an easily overlooked looseness
The per-root test is the change of the Ritz value between two consecutive Davidson iterations, below ethr (note 1). Two additional facts relevant for work accounting:
- In SCF mode, after the Davidson call returns, up to five roots may remain unconverged without any consequence; only when more than 5 roots (the larger of 5 and a quarter of the bands is used for the warning) are unconverged is the solver called again, at most 5 more times. So the effective accuracy of the density can be worse than ethr.
- The change of a Ritz value overstates the remaining error when convergence is fast and understates it when convergence is slow: with a contraction factor q per step the remaining error is about q/(1-q) times the last change. At q = 0.1 the remaining error is about one ninth of the change; at q = 0.9 it is nine times the change. So the real accuracy delivered per unit of ethr varies by an order of magnitude with the convergence rate. This is relevant for the experiments below because it means ethr cannot be mapped to a residual norm by a simple formula.

### 5.5 Convergence declaration
Converged means dr2 < conv_thr at the iteration just completed, using the output density of that diagonalization. When converged the potential is recomputed from the output density, the descf term is dropped, and the energy printed is evaluated at the output density (so the energy has second-order error in the residual). There is no separate fixed-point certificate, no check that the eigenvalues were converged, and no gap check. The last-iteration threshold is whatever the ratchet gave (5.9e-11 Ry on si8, already including the division by the 32 electrons).

## 6. Energy evaluation

QE assembles E = eband + deband + (E_xc - E_xc,core) + E_Ewald + E_H + descf (+ smearing terms, none here).
- eband is the sum of occupied eigenvalues (weights times Ritz values) of the Hamiltonian built from the INPUT density of this iteration.
- deband (computed after the diagonalization) is minus the integral of the OUTPUT density times the INPUT Hartree+xc potential. Together, eband + deband equals the kinetic plus ionic (local and nonlocal) energy of the output orbitals. No separate evaluation of the nonlocal or kinetic energy is needed: it is hidden in the eigenvalue sum.
- E_H and E_xc are the values obtained in the potential construction from the MIXED density that becomes the next input.
- descf = minus the integral of (mixed density minus output density) times the Hartree+xc potential of the mixed density: the first-order Taylor correction that turns the sum "E_H[mixed] + E_xc[mixed]" into an estimate of "E_H[out] + E_xc[out]" with error of second order in (out - mixed). So the printed energy of a non-converged iteration is an energy of the OUTPUT orbitals and density, correct to second order in their difference from the mixed density, assembled with only dot products and the potential evaluation that the next iteration needs anyway.
- hwf_energy, the Harris-Weinert-Foulkes energy: eband + deband computed with the PREVIOUS input potential's double counting, evaluated entirely at the input density. It is printed beside the total energy and, on the way to convergence, the pair brackets the converged energy.
No extra FFT, no extra Hartree, no separate kinetic or nonlocal loop. This is the main structural difference with BAANDI's per-iteration audit (section 7).

## 8. The error estimate and threshold coupling in BAANDI's terms

### 8.1 Definitions (Hartree atomic units)
Let n_in and n_out be the input and output densities on the real-space grid with N_r points and cell volume Omega, with Fourier coefficients defined by n(r) = sum_G n~(G) exp(i G.r), i.e. n~(G) = (1/N_r) sum_r n(r) exp(-i G.r), unit e/Bohr^3. Let Delta n = n_out - n_in and let S be the set of reciprocal vectors of the density sphere (the same set the mixer would use). Define

E_res = (Omega / 2) * sum over G in S, G != 0 of 4 pi |Delta n~(G)|^2 / |G|^2,

with unit Hartree (Bohr^3 times Bohr^-2 times (e/Bohr^3)^2 equals e^2/Bohr = Ha). It is the Hartree energy of the residual density, and equals the Hartree energy returned by the existing `hartree::evaluate` applied to Delta n if the sum is restricted to S and the G=0 term is dropped (as it already is). QE's dr2 is the same quantity in Ry: dr2 = 2 E_res.

The relationship between the two energy scales is unit-covariant: the QE threshold 0.1 * dr2 / N in Ry is 0.1 * E_res / N in Ha, with a cap 1e-2 Ry = 5e-3 Ha and a floor 1e-13 Ry = 5e-14 Ha.

Let N_e be the number of valence electrons, N_occ = N_e/2 the number of doubly occupied bands in the unpolarized case. Define the target eigenvalue-error scale for iteration k

epsilon_k = min( epsilon_{k-1}, 0.1 * E_res,k-1 / max(1, N_e) )   (Ha, ratcheted; epsilon_1 = 5e-3 Ha),

and the first-iteration redo condition E_res,1 < epsilon_1 * max(1, N_e), in which case epsilon_1 := 0.1 * E_res,1 / max(1, N_e) and iteration 1 is solved once more from the vectors just obtained.

### 8.2 Translating epsilon into BAANDI's residual criterion
BAANDI's eigensolver stops when a band's residual norm is below a tolerance tau (Ha). The standard bound for a Ritz pair with residual r_b and a gap g to the rest of the spectrum is that the eigenvalue error is at most ||r_b||^2 / g, and the angle to the exact vector is at most ||r_b|| / g. For the density only the occupied subspace matters, so g is the gap between the highest occupied Ritz value and the lowest Ritz value above it (a quantity that BAANDI already knows, since the first empty band is solved strictly and the sampled gap is checked). Splitting the total error budget epsilon_k evenly over the occupied electrons gives the energy-matched residual tolerance

tau_k = sqrt( g_k * epsilon_k / N_e ),   with the sum over occupied bands of f_b ||r_b||^2 / g_k <= epsilon_k when every band meets tau_k (f_b = 2).

Units: g in Ha, epsilon in Ha, tau in Ha. Caveats: g_k should come from the previous iteration's Ritz values (known), and is floored to avoid a zero for systems with a tiny gap, in which case the estimate is not a certificate (the gap check already flags those). This is a mapping, not a proven equivalence. It differs from QE (which compares the Ritz-value change) and its numeric effect on BAANDI's stopping point is not known: using the sampled-gap values reported in `scf-mixing-comparison.md` (0.008 to 0.30 Ha for the studied cases, 0.0785 Ha for the Gamma Si case) and the relayed si8 numbers, tau from this formula lands within a factor of about five of the current rule (0.1 times the L2 residual) in the middle iterations, in either direction depending on the gap, and is not systematically looser. No more than that should be inferred before measuring.

BAANDI's current rule is tau = clamp(0.1 * L2-residual_{k-1}, 1e-9, 1e-2) with the normalized L2 residual in Bohr^-3/2 per electron: it is linear in the density error, has no gap dependence, is not a ratchet, and is based on a norm in which all G components weigh equally.

### 8.3 What would have to hold to preserve the SCF iteration count
The P14A result (any fixed loosening factor above one increases the SCF count; factor 3 gives 16 instead of 15 on si8, 12 instead of 10 on k444s, a divergence on the 20-atom skewed fixture) means that the present schedule sits close to the limit of what the Pulay mixer tolerates in its present metric. The following conditions are necessary for a new schedule to keep the count; they are conditions to check, not proven sufficient:
1. The diagonalization noise in the output density must stay a fixed small fraction of the SCF residual in the metric the mixer minimizes: ||delta n_diag|| <= kappa ||Delta n|| at every iteration with kappa of order 0.1. The Hartree-metric version of the same statement is sqrt(E_diag) <= kappa sqrt(E_res). QE's rule with N_e = 32 and factor 0.1 budgets a total diagonalization energy error of about 0.1 E_res / 32 per unit of eigenvalue error, i.e. about 0.003 E_res; the corresponding kappa in norm is not derived here and is to be measured (E3).
2. The noise level must change slowly from iteration to iteration. The Pulay history combines residuals from several iterations, so a sudden tightening makes old residuals relatively noisy. The ratchet guarantees monotone tightening; BAANDI's schedule follows the residual up and down. Which of the two is better for the mixer is an open empirical question (E5).
3. The metric of the mixer should agree with the metric of the estimate, so that the quantity driving the schedule is also the quantity the least-squares step is trying to reduce (E4).
4. The last iterations must keep the strict tolerance required by the certificate; the energy-matched rule must be floored at the existing 0.1 * eigen_tolerance, and the existing fixed-point certificate must be unchanged. The rule may only change the path to convergence, not the stopping gate.
5. A better starting point (density and vectors) is independent of the above: it reduces work at the front of the run, where QE's early iterations cost 3 to 4 steps instead of 9 to 10.

### 8.4 Experiments (all behavior-preserving first)
E0 (diagnostic only, no change of numerics): compute E_res (Hartree metric, density sphere only, G=0 excluded) every SCF iteration and store it next to the existing L2 residual; add the Hartree-metric norm of the density difference between consecutive iterations. Expected result: a table of E_res versus the L2 residual versus total-energy error against the converged value for each iteration. Systems: the four P14A systems plus the P10G benchmark set (cubic, skewed, elongated, slab-like cells, 1 to 20 atoms, small and large gaps, Gamma and k meshes).
E1 (equal-accuracy comparison): from E0, find the first iteration where E_res < 5e-9 Ha (equal to QE's 1e-8 Ry convergence threshold). Compare BAANDI's total-energy error at that iteration and the iteration number with the BAANDI final (certified) result and with QE's 14 iterations at 1e-8. This says how much of the SCF count and Davidson work difference at "1e-8" is a difference in what was demanded. It should be reported before anything else: if BAANDI's certificate demands several orders more than QE's, the headline ratio of 3.3 times more Davidson steps per SCF iteration is partly definition.
E2 (offline replay of stopping rules, no solver change): record for each Davidson step of the current runs the per-band residual norms and the Ritz-value changes, then replay candidate stopping rules (current residual rule; Ritz-change below epsilon_k; the energy-matched tau_k of 8.2; with and without the ratchet) and count which step each would have stopped at. Davidson's trajectory is identical up to the stopping point, so this predicts the work saved per iteration without running new SCF cycles. It does not predict the effect on the SCF count.
E3 (calibration of kappa): for each SCF iteration, solve the same potential twice, once at the production tolerance and once at a much tighter tolerance, and record the Hartree-metric and L2 norms of the density difference divided by those of Delta n. Do this for tolerance multipliers 1, 3, 10 (the P14A sweep) to see at which kappa the SCF count starts to rise. This turns "factor 3 failed" into a measured threshold on kappa, to which any new rule can then be held.
E4 (mixer metric A/B): replace the L2 inner products of the Pulay Gram matrix and right-hand side with the Hartree-metric inner products over the density sphere (history stored in G space, G=0 removed), leaving beta, history length and tolerances at their present values; then repeat the P14A factor sweep. Report SCF counts for the benchmark set and the number of history resets. A pure mixer change; bit-identical results are neither expected nor required; the converged energy must agree to 1e-12 Ha.
E5 (ratchet): with the existing L2-residual rule, apply min over history; then with the energy-matched rule; compare SCF counts and Davidson steps.
E6 (first-iteration redo): count how often it would fire on the benchmark set from the uniform start, from an atomic start (E7) and from a restart density.
E7 (atomic start density): requires parsing the atomic valence charge from the pseudopotential file; build the starting density as in section 2.2 with BAANDI's own radial-to-G transform and a deterministic safeguard (if the real-space density is negative beyond a stated floor, fall back to the uniform start). Compare SCF counts and the converged energy (must agree to 1e-12 Ha since the fixed point is independent of the start).
E8 (starting wavefunctions): (a) in the first iteration, a Rayleigh-Ritz rotation in a free-electron subspace twice the band count before Davidson (no pseudopotential data needed); (b) atomic wavefunctions plus small random noise in a subspace of size max(atomic functions, bands). Count Davidson steps in iterations 1 to 3.
E9 (mixer resets): summarize `mixing_resets` and `mixer_reset` over the benchmark set and the P10D matrix; if resets occur, test replacing the reset by a damped step that keeps the history.
E10 (non-eigensolver phase budget): per-phase wall time and FFT counts for si8 and one k-point case with the existing timers, separately for the density phase, the Hartree call for input and output, the audit and the mixing; compare with the QE per-iteration figures (sum_band about 5 ms, v_of_rho 2 ms, mix_rho 3 ms on si8) and with the FFT-count estimate of section 3.3.

## 9. Hypotheses for BAANDI

Ranking is by expected value per risk. Each states the idea, expected gain and its basis, the risk, the validation gate and conflicts with the ledger. Every gate includes the standing rules: converged energy within 1e-12 Ha of the strict reference, SCF count and Davidson work reported on the whole benchmark set (not silicon alone), fixed-point certificate unchanged, no default changed on one system's evidence (Generality rule).

1. Hartree-metric Pulay least squares (E0, E4), with the Hartree-weighted residual norm E_res reported as a diagnostic.
   - Idea: take the inner products of the Pulay Gram matrix and right-hand side from the Hartree kernel over the density sphere with G=0 excluded (a G-space history over about a quarter of the grid points), keeping the update formula, history, beta and the charge constraint. Add E_res to the iteration record.
   - Expected gain: unknown size; SCF count from 0 to 3 fewer iterations on small cases (one iteration is about 6.7 percent of the work on si8 at 15 iterations), and, more importantly, increased tolerance of the mixer to diagonalization noise, which is the thing the P14A sweep showed to be missing. Basis: QE's identical fit in this metric converges si8 in 14 iterations with eigensolver thresholds that BAANDI's mixer could not accept in the L2 metric; the mechanism (suppression of high-G noise in the fit) is argued in 4.2, not measured. Mixing time itself falls by about half, but is 0.1 to 1 percent of the run.
   - Risk: low in complexity; moderate in outcome, since the metric emphasizes long wavelength components and may need a larger history in slab-like cells.
   - Validation gate: SCF count not higher than the current count on any benchmark system and lower on at least some; the P14A sweep rerun with the new metric; the 20-atom skewed fixture (diverges at factors 3 and 10) is a required case.
   - Ledger: touches only the mixing module and the scheduling input; does not conflict with the P14A decision (it does not loosen anything by itself). Distinct from the Kerker preconditioning studied in P10D (a preconditioner on the step, rejected as default); this changes the least-squares metric.

2. Energy-weighted, ratcheted schedule with first-iteration redo (E0 to E6). This is the old H6, now with a concrete formula.
   - Idea: epsilon_k from E_res of the previous iteration with the 0.1/N_e factor, monotone, converted to a residual tolerance through the gap-based mapping of 8.2, with the first-iteration redo; the final strict tolerance and the certificate unchanged.
   - Expected gain: the largest of all (QE's 83 versus BAANDI's 290 to 340 Davidson steps; the diagonalization is 93 percent of si8 SCF time and P14A buffer-origin work is about 25 percent). If a quarter to half of the gap were closed, 15 to 40 percent of the Davidson steps would go. Basis: relayed QE iteration counts; the 1 to 2 step iterations explained by the ratchet (5.2).
   - Risk: high. P14A shows a plain loosening increases SCF counts; the mapping between ethr and a residual tolerance is not unique (5.4) and may make the schedule tighter than today in the middle iterations. Only after hypothesis 1 and the calibration E3.
   - Validation gate: Davidson-step total lower AND SCF count equal, on at least 8 systems of varied shape, gap, band count and k mesh; the P14A result stays valid as a bound for fixed-factor schedules, so a candidate must beat the factor-1 work at equal count, per the ticket's acceptance rule.
   - Ledger: conflicts with the P14A "no tested factor preserves SCF count" if implemented as a looser factor; it is a different family (metric, ratchet, gap mapping) and must be reported as such. Gated by P14A's own conditional note on H6.

3. Starting density from atomic charge superposition (E6, E7).
   - Idea: parse the atomic valence charge from the pseudopotential file; build the starting density per shell; renormalize; uniform fallback when the section is missing or the superposition is too negative.
   - Expected gain: 1 to 3 SCF iterations on cases where the uniform start is far (a covalent crystal), also cheaper first diagonalization. Basis: QE starts here; BAANDI's first iteration residual 0.026 per electron is removed by mixing. Magnitude unmeasured.
   - Risk: low to moderate: UPF with no atomic-charge section, ringing negative density (BAANDI forbids clipping), parser change in `dft-upf`.
   - Validation gate: same converged energy within 1e-12 Ha on all systems; no extra failures; SCF counts not higher anywhere.
   - Ledger: not an overhead-only change, so the bit-identical rule does not apply; the energy and the certified fixed point must. The restricted fixture UPFs (Gaussian ions) need a synthetic atomic charge to keep the benchmark set generic.

4. Better starting wavefunctions (E8).
   - Idea: Rayleigh-Ritz in a larger starting subspace before the first Davidson call (free-electron subspace of twice the band count; later atomic pseudo-wavefunctions).
   - Expected gain: a few Davidson steps (up to about 12 to 17 of 340 on si8, from the 9/10 versus 3/4 pattern in iterations 1 and 2); 3 to 5 percent. Basis: relayed QE counts, with the caveat that part of the gap is the criterion.
   - Risk: low for the free-electron version (no new data), moderate for atomic (new radial transform, parser).
   - Gate: Davidson steps down in iterations 1 to 3 on all systems, converged results identical.
   - Ledger: O4c (free-electron start) measured an improvement against random; this is a further improvement of the same decision, not a reversal.

5. Avoid history-destroying resets in the mixer (E9).
   - Idea: survey first; if resets occur, replace the reset by a damped step or a smaller history trim.
   - Expected gain: unknown; zero if resets never occur. Risk: low. Gate: survey, then SCF count. Ledger: the ADR forbids per-sample clipping, not this.

6. Equal-accuracy convergence profile (E1; needs the user's decision).
   - Idea: expose, in addition to the present strict certificate, a documented "standard" profile whose declaration of convergence is the Hartree-metric E_res threshold, the energy of the output density with first-order descf correction, and a single final certificate pass only on request.
   - Expected gain: E1 will tell; the last iterations on si8 cost 54, 21, 32 steps (BAANDI) and the strict floor makes them the most expensive. Risk: this changes what "converged" means, which AGENTS.md treats as a user-level decision, and "do not relax tolerances to make a test pass" applies; it must be an additional, labelled profile, never a silent relaxation. Gate: the user approves; the strict default unchanged. Ledger: none if additive.

7. Cheaper energy evaluation in non-final iterations.
   - Idea: use the QE-style second-order energy (eigenvalue sum, double counting, potential energies from the mixed density, plus the first-order correction) for the iteration log and the energy-change test, and run the full audit (extra Hartree and xc of the output density, per-band nonlocal energy, remainder bounds) only for the converged iteration and the certificate.
   - Expected gain: removes about one extra Hartree (two transforms), the local-energy transform and a per-band nonlocal apply per iteration; 3 to 5 percent of the Gamma run in the phase record (energy 0.007 of 0.137 s), much less on si8. Risk: low in numbers, moderate in semantics because the convergence test uses `energy_change`. Gate: identical final energies and certificates; per-iteration energies not claimed identical. Ledger: the audit is a validated certificate; it must remain exactly as is at the end.

8. Hartree, density and gradient kernels: cache the |G|^2 or kernel array once, loop over the density sphere, use real-to-complex transforms for real fields, and count FFTs in GGA (about twelve now against eight).
   - Expected gain: small in seconds today (potential 0.005 s of 0.137 s at Gamma) but required for parity at QE speed; r2c halves transform work for real fields. Risk: low; the caching variant is bit-identical, r2c changes rounding at the 1e-16 level (energy gate 1e-12). Gate: bench_scf equivalence plus per-phase timings (E10). Ledger: none; optimization-log rules apply.

9. Comparison-configuration items that change what "QE speed" means (not implementation items here): the real-wavefunction Gamma mode (about 2 times fewer FFTs and half-length vectors), QE's default crystal symmetry for multi-k (far fewer than the relayed 32 k points on k444s if the symmetry is on), and QE's default band count of occupied bands only for insulators. The relayed QE runs used the complex Gamma mode, nosym and 20 bands to match BAANDI; they are fair equal-footing numbers but not QE's best configuration. The orchestrator should decide whether the target is measured against the best configuration (AGENTS.md says so), in which case QE reference timings for these configurations are needed from dev08, and whether the symmetry reduction and a real-wavefunction Gamma mode enter the roadmap. Ledger: no code change; documentation of the target.
