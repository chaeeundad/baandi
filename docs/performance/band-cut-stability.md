# Band count at the truncation boundary: SCF stability scan (orchestrator, Mac, 2026-11-08)

Follow-up of P41A (docs/performance/trajectory-divergence.md: tight solves remove the SCF-count variance; the first loose solve leaves order-one occupied-subspace defects) and of the band-count scan (band-count-scan.md, P38A). Hypothesis tested: the instability comes from the band set ending INSIDE a degenerate multiplet (the last requested band is not separated from the next band). Method: the P29A perturbation harness (`crates/dft-engine/tests/standard_stall_trajectory.rs`, new environment variable `P29A_EXTRA_BANDS`, with `P29A_GAMMA_REAL=on` for the real path), 1e-10 relative start-density perturbations, 10 standard-profile starts plus 2 strict references, pure Rust release, T=1, Mac observation (SCF counts and H work are deterministic counts).

Reading (observation): (1) with the band set closed at a suitable boundary (si8 extra 8, si16 extra 12) all ten perturbed standard runs follow the SAME trajectory (SCF 10 in every run, the reference code's own count on si16), i.e. the instability disappears completely, and the work falls (si16 H median 1687 to 1151, -32 %; si8 SCF 25.5 to 10); (2) a different total band count can be much WORSE than the default (si16 extra 8: median 44.5, range 28 to 50, H work 2.4 times the default), consistent with the cut falling inside another multiplet; (3) the effect is not monotonic in the number of extra bands, so a fixed integer cannot be right for all cells, while the quantity that decides it is local to the top of the band set. This reopens the band-count policy of P38A with a different premise: P38A judged rules on H vectors in the strict profile (first-empty multiplet completion), whereas this scan judges the standard-profile SCF stability and H work on perturbation distributions and the multiplet at the truncation boundary. Not established: the strict-profile effect in general (strict SCF 13 to 15 versus 18 to 20 here), cells without symmetry degeneracies (k222 and the PseudoDojo Si are already stable at the default), the dev08 wall-time effect, and a rule that finds a good boundary without knowing the converged spectrum.

## Addendum: what the full P42A scan shows (orchestrator reading of `P42A-evidence/summary.csv`, 2026-11-08)

P42A (docs/performance/band-boundary-policy.md; 8,112 starts) found that the converged gap at the last requested band is neither necessary nor sufficient (si8 complex extra 8 is stable although the boundary gap is 8.5e-12 Ha), so the multiplet-cut hypothesis above is NOT the mechanism. What the table does show: instability at the default of four extra bands is confined to the Gamma-only cells with many occupied bands; and for those the SCF distribution collapses to one trajectory once the number of extra bands exceeds a threshold that grows with the number of occupied bands. Per case and path, smallest extra from which every larger count is stable (at least 8 successful starts, at most 2 distinct SCF counts); occupied bands in brackets: si8 Gamma complex 6 (16 occupied; extra 6 is 10/10/11, extra 7 is 9 in all starts), si8 real 8, si16 Gamma complex and real 10 (32 occupied; extra 8 is the anomalous 39 to 48 SCF, 9 of 10 converged, boundary gap 6.0e-4 Ha), si-gamma, si-low, si-k222, si-k222s, si-k444s, si-skew, material Si and MgO and most synthetic fixtures (few occupied bands or k meshes) stable from 1 or 2 extras at the default already; never stable in the scanned range: syn-100-bands (100 bands, 121 to 151 SCF at the default), syn-gaussian-fcc (24 to 37), syn-s3 (complex), and the known non-converging elongated and slab cells. For the stable large cells the work also falls: si8 complex H median 1167.5 at the default to 585.5 (extra 6) and 561 (extra 7); si16 complex 1854.5 to 1078 (extra 10). Hypothesis (exploratory, from one scan, not preregistered): extra bands of about 0.35 to 0.4 times the number of occupied bands remove the instability for Gamma-only cells; with occupied counts of 4 to 11 the default of four extra bands is unaffected by such a rule. The reference code is stable with nbnd equal to the occupied count, so the sensitivity is a property of BAANDI's solver (loose solves of the occupied subspace, P41A) that a larger buffer compensates, not of the physics. A fixed-fraction rule must be tested on systems not used for the scan (ticket P43A).
